# AAAI-27 Review — "Use LLMs to Build Repair Tools, Not Patch Generation: Cost-Effective Automated Program Repair"

**Reviewer:** R1 (APR / SE4AI / LLM-systems)
**Round:** 5

---

## 1. Summary

The paper argues that LLM-based repair of programming-assignment submissions is deployed in a cost-inefficient way: one query per submission, re-transmitting the assignment-invariant 77% of the prompt on every call, with prompts (439 tokens on average) too short for provider caching to engage. It proposes CoEP, which spends the LLM once per assignment: a builder agent receives the specification, test suite, 3 reference solutions, and 5 historical buggy submissions, and synthesizes an executable symbolic repairer (problem-specific AST rewrite rules over a generic bounded edit search), refined for up to 3 validation-feedback rounds, then frozen and run locally; every candidate patch is independently test-validated and capped at relative patch size (RPS) <= 0.35, and unrepaired submissions escalate to conventional per-submission LLM repair. On 5 assignments and 1,685 real CS1 submissions from the public Refactory dataset (n=200 held out for the LLM arms; full-corpus re-run for the local arm), the synthesized repairers alone fix 47.5% of held-out submissions (50.7% corpus-wide) at zero marginal LLM cost with zero unsafe outputs, and the hybrid preserves each fallback protocol's quality (identically 98.5% for the enforced-retry protocol) at a 1.8-1.9x smaller marginal cost, with a $0.256/assignment build cost and corpus-average break-even around 154-197 submissions. Eight baselines (plain/cached/few-shot/enforced-retry frontier repair, cheap model, cascade, generic-search-only, and the non-LLM Refactory tool) plus ablations (generic-only 6.5%, Haiku builder 10.0%) locate the power in the LLM-synthesized rules and frontier-level builder capability.

## 2. Strengths and Weaknesses

### Strengths

**S1. A genuinely under-examined framing, made concrete.** The "many-instances, one-specification" cost anatomy (Sec. 2) is specific and verifiable: 439-token prompts decomposed 241/99/99, 77% byte-identical across a class, only 55% contiguously cacheable, below the 1,024-token provider caching minimum. The observation that provider caching serves real redundancy "at the wrong granularity" is a crisp, correct point, and the paper is honest that caching does engage for the bloated few-shot prompt ($6.53 -> $2.91 x10^-3). This is a deployment-economics contribution, not another patch-generation technique, and it is a fresh angle relative to the per-submission AAAI-25/26 APR line.

**S2. The baseline suite includes the arm that could have killed the paper.** The "+ validator, 1 retry" enforcement-parity baseline (98.5% at $3.26 x10^-3) grants the per-submission paradigm CoEP's own enforcement mechanism, and the paper leads with the honest consequence: CoEP's contribution over it is cost only, with quality inheritance exact (CoEP+retry repairs the identical 197/200, discordant 0:0, at $1.78 x10^-3 — I verified the 1.83x ratio and the ~173-submission break-even). Most submissions in this space would not have run this arm. Refactory is likewise run in its strongest configuration with all 2,442 correct solutions, and its own oracle (91.0%) is reported alongside the harness's 90.5%, pre-empting a re-validation-artifact objection.

**S3. Statistical reporting is unusually candid.** Wilson CIs on every rate; exact McNemar with discordant counts disclosed (I verified 0:8 -> p=0.0078, 0:9 -> p=0.0039, 0:7 -> p=0.0156, 0:82 -> p=4.1e-25); the Discussion explicitly concedes that hybrid-vs-baseline tests are structurally one-sided (the hybrid contains the fallback) and that the cluster-level sign test over 5 assignments (3 wins, 0 losses, 2 ties) is not significant, downgrading the headline quality edge to "suggestive." The load-bearing claims are correctly re-anchored on the symmetric comparisons.

**S4. The safety property is real and the right one for education.** Across 200 attempts the local path emitted zero test-failing and zero over-cap patches; failure is abstention. The never-trust-self-reports design (harness independently re-executes every patch; synthesized code contractually cannot return unvalidated patches) is the correct architecture for LLM-written tools in a grading pipeline, and the generate-validate-refine loop demonstrably matters (3 of 5 builders shipped delivery defects, e.g., ast.unparse reformatting blowing the cap 0/5 -> 5/5, caught and fixed by feedback).

**S5. Ablations isolate the source of power.** Generic-only search collapses to 6.5% (13/200) vs. 47.5%, so synthesized rules account for 86% (82/95) of local repairs; a Haiku-tier builder collapses to 10.0% with 14/25 build success — together these substantiate "synthesis is a frontier-capability task bought once" and rule out the cheap explanation that the operator library does the work. The 33% near-duplicate patch-retrieval bound vs. 50.7% rule coverage rebuts the obvious "just cache patches" alternative.

**S6. Numbers are internally consistent under adversarial checking.** I recomputed the cost model ($2.73 x10^-3 base; $0.256/assignment build from 51,227+75,015 tokens; break-evens 197/154/295/173; builder-upgrade break-even ~229; few-shot 2.39x; cascade adverse-selection gap $1.94 vs. naive $1.86 consistent with the reported 67% frontier success on escalated instances) and found essentially no errors. The full-corpus re-run (50.7% [48.3, 53.1] on 1,660) tightens the headline local-coverage estimate beyond the n=200 subsample.

### Weaknesses

**W1. The demonstrated economic stakes are cents, and the paper's own numbers say so.** At the paper's prices, repairing this corpus's average 337 submissions/assignment with the plain frontier baseline costs $0.92 per assignment per offering; fronting with CoEP saves ~$0.44 per assignment per offering (~$2.60/course/term across five assignments vs. the retry protocol). The paradigm argument therefore rests entirely on extrapolation — to MOOC volumes, richer protocols, and rate-limit/latency/privacy concerns (asserted, not measured: no deadline-spike experiment, no latency distribution beyond a mean). A slope-vs-intercept argument is structurally sound, but at AAAI I expect the demonstrated regime to carry more of the weight. The paper is upfront that the claim is "a 1.8-2.5x smaller slope plus an intercept, not zero marginal cost," which I credit, but significance is capped by the demonstrated scale.

**W2. The headline quality win over the deployed baseline is small, structurally one-sided, and not cluster-significant.** 90.0% vs. 86.0% is 8 discordant instances out of 200; the hybrid cannot lose by construction (fallback responses are the baseline's own generations, per the Discussion), so p=0.008 tests only "at least one local win exists." At the honest unit of analysis (5 assignments) the paper concedes non-significance. Yet the abstract, introduction, and RQ1 box all foreground this comparison ("repairs strictly more," "exceeds every deployed single-shot configuration"), with the caveat deferred to the Discussion. The defensible headline is cost-at-equal-quality; the quality-edge framing oversells.

**W3. Internal framing inconsistencies in the break-even and speedup claims.** (a) The RQ2 box claims break-even "inside one offering for four of five assignments," but the same subsection's per-assignment analysis yields three of five (sort_age N*=368 vs. 297 submissions = 1.2 offerings; top_k 704 vs. 108). The 4/5 figure holds only under corpus-average per-submission savings, which the paper's own per-assignment computation supersedes. (b) The "1.8-2.5x" range (RQ2 box, Conclusion) mixes same-protocol fronting (1.81-1.91x, verified) with a cross-protocol substitution (plain frontier -> CoEP+cascade, 2.55x); the Conclusion attaches the range to "matches the enforced-retry protocol," where the actual ratio is 1.83x. Neither is fatal, but both inflate in the same direction.

**W4. Local coverage is under half and highly heterogeneous, so the "new paradigm" routes the majority of traffic through the old one.** 47.5% held-out (27.9-71.4% per assignment); the local path's accepted patches are also slightly less minimal than the frontier's on shared instances (0.115 vs. 0.093 RPS), so the local arm wins on enforcement, not patch quality. Whether 5 build bugs is the right operating point is not explored empirically (no build-set-size sweep), despite the framework "pricing extensions explicitly."

**W5. Suite overfitting runs against the pedagogical use case, and the mitigation is unevaluated.** The hidden-oracle audit is commendable, but its result is adverse: 92.6% of accepted local patches agree with the reference on all 60 probes vs. 97.1% for the baseline — i.e., ~7.4% of local "repairs" (the artifact shown to a student as their corrected program) diverge from reference behavior off-suite, a 4.5pp differential the local path itself causes by optimizing the visible suite. The proposed fix (probe as a third validator) is mentioned but not run as the deployed configuration; deploying it would presumably lower the 47.5% coverage and shift every break-even. For a feedback tool this is the single most decision-relevant unmeasured number.

**W6. External validity is thin for a paradigm claim.** Five assignments, one course, one language, CS1-scale programs where functional repair is saturated (every LLM arm at 100% test-pass), one model family, one builder seed per assignment (multi-seed replication "mechanical" but not performed), n=40/assignment for LLM arms with ±5-7pp CIs against 4-4.5pp effect sizes. The RQ3 finding that even a Haiku builder fails at CS1 scale cuts both ways: it supports the frontier-capability claim but suggests fragility as assignment complexity grows — precisely the regime (larger programs, longer prompts, caching engaged) where the economics would be most interesting and where no evidence is offered. Refactory's original benchmark scale is comparable, but the AAAI-25 counterexample-guided line evaluates broader IPA corpora.

**W7. Costs are modeled, not billed, with a uniform chars/3.6 tokenizer.** The paper handles this about as well as possible (raw strings released; ±20% differential-error bound stated), but every dollar figure and break-even in the paper is downstream of one approximate constant applied to code-heavy vs. prose-heavy payloads whose true tokenization ratios differ systematically; no spot-calibration against a real tokenizer is reported. Relatedly, CoEP+frontier's $1.43 x10^-3 equals the naive 0.525 x 2.73 product to three digits, while the cascade and CoEP+retry rows carry adverse-selection premiums — either a coincidence worth explaining or a hint that this row was computed differently than claimed ("measured per-instance sums").

**W8. Novelty is a recombination.** Per-assignment amortization is the founding insight of CLARA/Refactory (acknowledged); LLM-written reusable tools are Voyager/LATM; self-refinement is cited as such. The contribution is the instantiation (verifiable, quality-constrained, hybrid-backed) plus the economics. The closest current competitor — AAAI-26's retrieval-guided learner-tailored repair — is engaged only by analogy ("our PyDex-style arm bounds this design's economics"), which assumes retrieval lengthens prompts the way five example pairs do; a retrieval index could instead select fewer, shorter exemplars.

## 3. Questions for the Authors

1. **Fallback reuse.** Are hybrid-arm fallback responses the stored generations from the corresponding standalone arm (making inheritance exact by construction), or fresh calls? If fresh calls, how is "identical 197/200" (discordant 0:0) preserved under sampling stochasticity; if reused, please state this explicitly in the Protocol, since "verified instance by instance" currently reads as an empirical finding rather than a near-tautology.
2. **Break-even framing.** Reconcile the RQ2 box ("inside one offering for four of five assignments") with the per-assignment computation in the same subsection (three of five: sort_age 368 vs. 297). Which is the intended claim, and will the box be corrected?
3. **Probe validator deployed.** If the 60-probe differential-testing check is added as a third validator (as the Discussion suggests deployments should), what are the resulting local coverage, hybrid quality, and break-evens? This is the configuration I would actually deploy for student-facing feedback; the paper should report it.
4. **Builder variance.** For at least two assignments (one easy, e.g., search; one hard, e.g., top_k), what is the spread of local coverage across >=3 builder seeds? One stochastic trajectory per assignment leaves the 47.5% headline without any variance estimate.
5. **CoEP+frontier pricing.** Why does the CoEP+frontier row show no adverse-selection premium ($1.43 ~= 0.525 x 2.73 exactly) when cascade ($1.94 vs. $1.86 naive) and CoEP+retry ($1.78 vs. $1.71 naive) do? Please confirm it is a per-instance sum and explain the coincidence.
6. **Tokenizer calibration.** Did you validate chars/3.6 against any real tokenizer on a sample of your actual payloads (code vs. prose separately)? What is the measured differential error between the builder's code-heavy outputs and the baselines' prompt-heavy inputs, and how does it move the 154-295 break-even band?
7. **Scaling the regime.** For one assignment with a substantially longer handout (>1,024 prompt tokens, so caching engages) and larger programs, does the builder still synthesize a useful repairer, and does the cached per-submission baseline then dominate? Even a single data point outside CS1 would materially strengthen the paradigm claim.
8. **Refactory port fidelity.** Does your Python 3.11 port reproduce the tool's originally published repair rates on this dataset (its oracle's 91.0% here vs. the 2019 paper's reported numbers), and was its RPS measured with your token-level Levenshtein or its original notion of patch size when you report 0.386?

## 4. Significance

**Score: 3/4.** The question — whether LLM reasoning for many-instances/one-specification workloads should be amortized into verifiable executable artifacts rather than paid per query — is real, timely, and transfers beyond APR (the rubric/hint extension is plausible). The inheritance result (identical quality set at 1.8x lower slope, with a closed-form per-assignment break-even an instructor can actually evaluate) is a useful decision procedure, not just a benchmark number. Significance is capped, not eliminated, by W1/W6: the demonstrated savings are cents per assignment on 5 CS1 problems, the local path covers under half of traffic, and the regime where the economics would bite (MOOC scale, richer assignments) is argued rather than evidenced.

## 5. Justification of the Approach

**Score: 3/4.** The architecture is well-motivated end to end: the cost anatomy justifies amortization; the never-trust-self-reports validation and abstention-only failure mode are exactly right for LLM-synthesized code in a grading loop and are empirically borne out (zero unsafe patches; refinement rescuing 3/5 builders from delivery defects); the hybrid guarantees coverage is never sacrificed; ablations justify the two design pillars (synthesized rules: 86% of local repairs; frontier builder: 47.5% vs. 10.0%). Falling short of a 4: key operating-point choices (k=5 build bugs, R_max=3, single builder sample) are fixed without sensitivity analysis; the suite-overfitting exposure inherent to optimizing the visible oracle is diagnosed but the identified remedy is not integrated; and the approach's justification presumes the fallback can be frozen/replayed (Q1), which real deployments cannot.

## 6. Quality of the Evaluation

**Score: 3/4.** Above the bar for this subfield in rigor: same-model paradigm control, eight baselines including the adversarial enforcement-parity arm, uniform validation and cost accounting, Wilson CIs, exact McNemar with disclosed discordants (all p-values I recomputed check out), tau-sweep, hidden-oracle audit, full-corpus confirmation of the local estimate, and candid disclosure of the cluster-level non-significance and the one-sided test structure. Held back from a 4 by breadth and by self-inflicted framing: 5 assignments/one course/one language/one model family with a single builder seed; n=40/assignment against 4pp effects; modeled rather than billed costs under one approximate tokenizer; no human or instructor assessment of feedback quality (the primary metric is entirely a threshold on patch size once test-pass saturates); and the W3 inconsistencies (4/5 vs. 3/5 break-even; 1.8-2.5x range) that consistently round in the paper's favor.

## 7. Reproducibility

**Score: 3/4.** Strong on paper: harness, import pipeline, prompts, synthesized repairers, all raw LLM strings for both builder tiers, seeds, timeouts, tau, pricing snapshot, and per-round build transcripts are stated as released; local search and evaluation are deterministic; the checklist is completed non-evasively. Deductions: regeneration depends on proprietary "July 2026 snapshot" models identified by tier rather than pinned model ID in the text; builder synthesis is stochastic with one trajectory per assignment and no seed-replication study; decoding parameters (temperature) are deferred to the released configuration rather than stated; dollar figures depend on the chars/3.6 convention rather than billed tokens. Analysis-level reproduction appears fully supported; experiment-level reproduction is model-access-contingent, as is typical.

## 8. Ethical Considerations

No serious concerns. The data are public, anonymized student submissions from a standard corpus; no new human-subjects data are collected; the system is positioned as instructor-supervised feedback, not autonomous grading. One substantive point the ethics statement elides: the hidden-oracle audit implies ~7% of locally produced "repairs" may pass the visible suite while diverging from the reference off-suite — showing a student a subtly wrong "corrected" program is a pedagogical harm specific to this system's design, and the mitigation (probe validator) should be deployed and evaluated, not just mentioned (see Q3). The equity implication of a two-tier pipeline (half of students served by a symbolic tool, half by a frontier model) is unexamined but the escalation design plausibly bounds it.

## 9. Overall Evaluation

**Score: 6/10 (marginal accept).**

This is a carefully executed, unusually honest systems paper making a deployment-paradigm argument the APR community should hear, with an evaluation whose internal rigor (enforcement-parity baseline, symmetric same-model design, disclosed discordants, full-corpus check, adverse hidden-oracle audit reported against interest) exceeds most accepted work in this line, including the recent AAAI-25/26 per-submission papers it positions against. Every headline number I checked is arithmetically consistent. What keeps it at marginal rather than clear accept: the demonstrated economic stakes are literally cents at the evaluated scale, so the central claim rests on extrapolation; the quality edge over the deployed baseline is 8 instances, one-sided by construction, and cluster-level non-significant while still being foregrounded; local coverage is under half with a measured 4.5pp off-suite-correctness deficit whose remedy is unevaluated; and the paradigm claim is supported by exactly one course, one language, one model family, and one builder seed per assignment. A revision that deploys the probe validator, fixes the W3 framing slips, adds builder-seed variance, and offers even one beyond-CS1 data point would move me to a 7.

**Confidence: 4/5.** I know this literature (Refactory/CLARA/Verifix, PyDex/MMAPR, ChatRepair, FrugalGPT, LATM/Voyager) and the Refactory corpus well, and I verified the paper's arithmetic directly; I have not run the released artifacts or inspected the synthesized repairers.
