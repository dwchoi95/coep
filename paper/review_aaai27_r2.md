# AAAI-27 Review — Reviewer 2 (ML systems / cost-efficient inference)

**Paper:** Use LLMs to Build Repair Tools, Not Patch Generation: Cost-Effective Automated Program Repair (CoEP)

---

## 1. Summary

The paper observes that programming-assignment repair is a "many-instances, one-specification" workload: per-submission LLM repair re-transmits the assignment description, tests, and instructions (79% of input tokens, per the authors' measurement) on every query. CoEP instead spends the LLM once per assignment: a builder agent reads the spec, tests, 3 reference solutions, and 5 sample buggy submissions, and synthesizes an executable Python repairer (assignment-specific AST rewrite rules over a generic bounded edit search), refined through at most 3 harness-driven rounds in which every patch is independently re-validated against the test suite and a relative-patch-size (RPS <= 0.35) cap. Deployed repairers run locally at zero marginal LLM cost; failures escalate to conventional per-submission LLM repair, whose cost is charged to CoEP. On 5 assignments from the public Refactory corpus (200 held-out real student submissions, 40 per assignment), the synthesized repairers alone fix 47.5%; the hybrid reaches 90.0-90.5% "quality repair rate" (test-pass AND RPS <= 0.35) versus 86.0% for the same-model per-submission baseline (exact McNemar on 0:8 discordant pairs, p = 0.008), with a one-off build cost of $0.256/assignment breaking even at roughly 154-295 submissions, inside one course offering for four of five assignments. Seven baselines (plain/cached/few-shot per-submission, Haiku, FrugalGPT-style cascade, generic-search-only, and the classical Refactory tool) and ablations (generic-only 6.5%, Haiku-builder 10.0%) round out the evaluation; costs are computed from message strings under a chars/3.6 tokenizer approximation at $3/$15 (Sonnet) and $1/$5 (Haiku) per Mtok.

## 2. Strengths and Weaknesses

### Strengths

**S1 — The amortization framing is crisp and the arithmetic is internally consistent.** The cost anatomy (Table: 439 input / 94 output tokens, 79% invariant), the closed-form break-even N* = C/Delta, and every derived number I checked reproduce from the released macros: 0.256/(2.73-1.43) ≈ 197, 0.256/(2.73-1.07) ≈ 154, 0.256/(1.94-1.07) ≈ 294, build $ = 51,227×$3/M + 75,015×$15/M ≈ $1.28/5 = $0.256, the "≈94 baseline repairs" equivalence, and the ≈230-submission cheap-vs-frontier builder upgrade break-even ((0.256-0.022)/(2.457-1.433) ≈ 229). This level of self-consistency is rare and appreciated.

**S2 — Sound safety-by-construction design.** The repairer can never self-report success; every patch is re-executed against the instructor tests plus the RPS check by an independent harness, escalation costs are counted against CoEP, and the abstention-only failure mode (zero test-failing, zero over-cap emissions in 200 attempts) is the right property for feedback shown to learners.

**S3 — Same-model paradigm control and unusually broad baseline coverage.** Holding the frontier model fixed across the per-submission baseline and the builder isolates the paradigm variable; the seven baselines span the three families a skeptic would demand (richer prompting, cheaper inference, classical symbolic), and the Refactory tool is run in its strongest configuration with 2,442 correct solutions — 300x more per-assignment data than CoEP receives.

**S4 — The ablations answer the right questions.** Generic-only (6.5%, discordant 0:82, p < 1e-24) cleanly attributes the local path to synthesized rules; the Haiku-builder rerun (10.0% vs 47.5%) and the round-by-round refinement forensics (15/25 -> 25/25 build success, with specific delivery defects like ast.unparse reformatting) are informative and candid.

**S5 — Honest limitations reporting.** Per-assignment heterogeneity (25-85%), the top_k low-traffic boundary case, the contamination discussion, and release of raw message strings for re-tokenization are all creditable.

### Weaknesses

**W1 — The headline quality-superiority claim is confounded by attempt budget; the McNemar test is structurally one-sided.** The hybrid is [validated local stage] + [the baseline itself as fallback]. Since the local stage emits only validated, cap-compliant patches and everything else receives exactly the baseline treatment, the hybrid cannot lose to the baseline on the primary metric — the 0:8 discordant split is guaranteed in direction by construction (the paper even says "the hybrid never does worse than the paradigm it replaces"). The p = 0.008 therefore tests only whether the local stage adds more than zero wins, not whether the *paradigm* is better. The fair comparison the paper never runs is a per-submission arm with the same validator and the same second attempt: frontier -> validate -> on cap violation, re-query (or re-prompt "minimize this patch"). The paper's own cascade result shows exactly this mechanism at work with no amortization at all: Haiku -> validate -> frontier reaches 88.5%, already 2.5pp above the single-attempt frontier baseline. A frontier -> validate -> frontier arm at ~2x marginal cost would plausibly close most of the 4pp gap; without it, "exceeds the per-submission baseline" attributes to amortization what may belong to validation-plus-retry. (The cost claim survives this confound; the quality claim does not, and "zero unsafe outputs" is likewise a property of wrapping any system in the validator, not of synthesis.)

**W2 — The significance story is fragile under multiplicity, and the comparisons needed for the strongest claims are untested.** The single significant headline test (p = 0.008) sits inside a family of at least seven arm comparisons implied by "exceeding every per-submission configuration" and "best on both axes": Bonferroni at 7 gives 0.056. More importantly, the claims actually foregrounded in the abstract and RQ2 box — CoEP+cascade (90.5%) as "the study's highest quality repair rate," beating the cascade (88.5%, a 4-submission difference) and PyDex-style (85.5%) — carry no tests at all and are transparently non-significant at n = 200 (all CIs overlap heavily: [85.6, 93.8] vs cascade's ~[83.5, 92.2]). "Dominant configuration" is not an established result; it is a point-estimate ordering.

**W3 — The primary metric is the constraint the proposed system enforces by construction, its threshold is unjustified, and the paper's robustness claim is contradicted by its own table.** Quality repair rate (test-pass AND RPS <= 0.35) is introduced by this paper; Refactory and PyDex *report* patch size, they do not threshold success at 0.35, so citing them as precedent for the cap is a stretch. All arms tie at ~100% test-pass; the entire result is manufactured by where tau falls relative to each arm's patch-size distribution, and no tau sweep is shown. The Discussion defends this with "sweeping it moves absolute rates but not orderings, since CoEP's patches are the smallest per arm" — but Table 1's own mean-RPS column (defined over each arm's cap-compliant repairs) ranks generic 0.023 < frontier 0.102 < PyDex 0.108 < Haiku 0.118 < CoEP-local 0.127: CoEP-local is sixth of eight. The RQ1 text's "smallest patches of any arm (mean RPS 0.127 vs. the frontier's 0.187)" compares CoEP's compliant-only mean against the frontier's mean over *all* test-passing patches including cap-busters — an apples-to-oranges denominator switch that reverses the like-for-like comparison. At smaller tau the ordering could plausibly flip toward the baseline; the robustness claim is asserted on a false premise.

**W4 — Cost accounting is advertised as exact but is an uncalibrated approximation over an emulated endpoint.** The contributions bullet promises "exact token-level cost accounting" and Metrics says "exact token counts of every prompt/response pair," yet the Protocol reveals chars/3.6 with no calibration against any real tokenizer ("provider tokenizers are unavailable offline" — the cited provider exposes a token-counting endpoint, and real API responses carry usage metadata, so this statement itself signals that no billable API was ever hit). All LLM calls ran "through an agent harness constrained to a stateless single-shot endpoint": (a) whatever scaffold/system prompt the harness injects is neither described nor counted, and behavior under a harness need not match behavior against the raw API being priced; (b) if the July-2026 "snapshots" use extended thinking, thinking tokens are billed as output but invisible to chars/3.6 — and would inflate the output-heavy build cost (88% of the $1.28 build is output tokens) most of all, directly attacking the break-even; (c) exact model IDs, temperature, and decoding parameters are never stated despite the checklist claiming they are; (d) the paper's own "±20% differential calibration error shifts break-even by at most the same factor" is loose — if C is undercounted 20% and Delta overcounted 20%, N* is off by 1.5x, not 1.2x; (e) even the anatomy table is sloppy: components sum to 432 against a stated total of 439.

**W5 — The cached baseline, as priced, cannot exist on the cited providers.** The cacheable contiguous prefix is 55% of 439 tokens ≈ 241 tokens. Anthropic prompt caching requires a minimum cacheable prefix of ~1024 tokens on Sonnet-class models (2048+ on Haiku-class), and OpenAI's automatic caching also requires 1024+ token prompts; a 439-token request cannot cache on either. So the 23% saving, the $2.10 m$ figure, and BreakEvenNCached = 257 describe an unimplementable configuration (the error is conservative for CoEP — real cache saving here is 0% — but a cost paper should get provider mechanics right). The 1.25x-write-once pricing also silently assumes an eternal cache; the default TTL is minutes, so semester-scale arrivals would re-write repeatedly. Ironically, the one arm that *could* cache is PyDex-style few-shot (1,493-token shared prefix > 1024), which would cut its 6.53 m$ to roughly 2.4 m$ and blunt both the "few-shot pays 2.4x for nothing" line and the caption's "richer prompt styles widen the gap" argument.

**W6 — Contamination symmetry is asserted, not established, and the direction of net bias is unknowable from this design.** The corpus — including the evaluation submissions and their students' own subsequent fixes — has been public since 2019, and these five assignments are analyzed in multiple published papers. Same-model does not imply same-benefit: the builder's task (enumerate the misconception classes of these specific, extensively documented assignments) is plausibly helped *more* by corpus-level memorization than repairing one specific file is; the showcased rules (>=2 vs !=1 in unique_day, the sort_age tuple accumulator) are exactly the kind of pattern that could be recalled rather than induced from 5 samples, which would inflate the "5 sample bugs suffice" narrative and the 47.5% local rate on fresh assignments. The rebuttals offered (refinement fixed formatting defects; Haiku collapsed to 10%) show delivery difficulty and capability sensitivity, not symmetry. One genuinely novel, post-cutoff assignment — cheap to add — would have settled this; none is attempted.

**W7 — Unit-of-analysis and sample-size problems.** All CIs and tests pool 200 submissions as iid, but they are clustered in 5 assignments with local rates from 25% to 85% — the intraclass correlation is plainly large. The pooled inference is valid only for "more submissions to these same five assignments"; for every generalizing claim (abstract, conclusion), the effective n is 5, and no cluster-robust or assignment-level analysis is given. Per-assignment estimates rest on n = 40 (±14-15pp), and the builder-refinement conclusions on 5 build bugs per assignment; the 100% build success (25/25) against 47.5% held-out is a large, undiscussed overfitting gap for the refinement loop, which optimizes build-set success directly. Only 200 of 1,685 available submissions are evaluated even though, at the paper's own prices, the full frontier-baseline corpus run costs about $5 — an odd economy for a cost paper that suggests the emulated harness, not the API meter, was the binding constraint. Finally, every arm uses a single stochastic decoding draw per instance with no repeated runs, so the run-to-run variance of the 4pp headline gap is unquantified, and the 98 submissions dropped at import (1,783 -> 1,685) are not characterized.

**W8 — Scope and overclaim at the margins.** Five CS1 assignments, ~92-token programs, one language, one model family; absolute stakes at the studied scale are milli-dollars (a full offering's frontier repair bill is ≈ $0.92 per assignment, < $5 for the whole corpus), so the practical motivation leans on latency/rate-limit/independence arguments that are asserted, not measured (no deadline-spike or throughput experiment). Minor overclaims: the abstract's "runs in milliseconds" vs measured mean 0.8-0.9 s (and the 0.8 vs 0.9 s inconsistency between the deployment text and Table 1); "holding the underlying LLM fixed across all conditions" (Haiku arms exist); and RQ3's top_k builder "emitted hand-verified corrected programs per structural pattern" — which sounds like a lookup table of canned replacement programs (with "hand-verified" unexplained in an allegedly automated pipeline) rather than generalizing rules.

## 3. Questions for the Authors

1. **Equal-budget control (decision-critical).** Run the per-submission frontier baseline with the same validator and one retry on cap violation (or a "minimize this patch" re-prompt). What are its quality rate and cost? Does CoEP+frontier still beat it on quality, or only on cost?
2. **Cache mechanics.** Given the ~241-token cacheable prefix and provider minimum cacheable-prefix lengths (≥1024 tokens on the cited Sonnet tier), how was the cached arm's 23% saving realizable at all? Please recompute the cached baseline and BreakEvenNCached under actual provider rules and state the assumed cache TTL/hit rate.
3. **Exact tokens.** You release raw strings — please report exact token counts via the provider's tokenizer/count-tokens endpoint for at least the build transcripts and the baseline arm. Did the "agent harness" inject a system prompt or produce thinking tokens, and were either counted? State exact model IDs and decoding parameters (temperature, top-p).
4. **Tau sweep and patch-size claim.** Provide quality-vs-tau curves for all arms, and reconcile "CoEP's patches are the smallest per arm" with Table 1 (frontier 0.102 vs CoEP-local 0.127 over cap-compliant repairs). On the 95 instances CoEP-local repairs, what is the paired mean RPS of CoEP vs the frontier baseline on those same instances?
5. **Multiplicity.** Which comparisons are confirmatory? Report exact McNemar (with discordant counts) for CoEP+cascade vs cascade (90.5 vs 88.5) and vs PyDex-style, and family-wise-adjusted p-values for the claim "exceeds every per-submission configuration."
6. **Fallback mechanics.** Does the hybrid replay the baseline arm's stored responses, or issue fresh queries? If fresh, under what temperature/seed, and is "the reverse never occurs" (0:8) then an empirical accident rather than a construction guarantee?
7. **Contamination probe.** Can you evaluate on even one assignment guaranteed absent from training data (post-cutoff or private), or perturb/paraphrase eval submissions to break verbatim recall, and report the local rate?
8. **Cluster-level inference.** Report assignment-level (n = 5) summaries with cluster-robust uncertainty for the headline deltas. How much of the 4pp hybrid-vs-baseline gap comes from a single assignment?
9. **Repairer anatomy.** For each of the five repairers: lines of code, number of rules, and what fraction of held-out repairs came from per-pattern canned replacement programs vs generalizing rewrite rules? What does "hand-verified" mean in RQ3?
10. **Why n = 40/assignment**, given a ~$5 full-corpus cost at your own prices — and will the camera-ready scale the main arms to all 1,685 submissions?

## 4. Significance

**Score: 3 (good).** The many-instances/one-specification framing and "amortize LLM reasoning into a verifiable executable artifact" recipe are genuinely useful reframings for an active AAAI subarea (the paper correctly situates itself against 2025 AAAI assignment-repair work), and the operational benefits (local, deterministic, auditable, no rate limits) are real. Significance is tempered by scale: at the studied workload the absolute savings are milli-dollars per submission and single-digit dollars per course, the demonstration covers five small CS1 assignments on a contaminated corpus, and the strongest quality mechanism (validate-then-escalate) is already known from cascade work.

## 5. Justification of the Approach

**Score: 3 (good).** The design is principled where it matters: independent re-validation with a never-trust-self-reports contract, abstention-not-error failure mode, escalation costs charged to the proposed method, and a closed-form break-even that makes the deployment decision explicit. Two justificatory gaps: the central attribution (quality gains from amortization rather than from validation-plus-second-attempt) is not established because the equal-budget control is missing (W1), and the primary success criterion is a threshold the method enforces by construction, chosen by the authors without precedent for the specific cap value (W3).

## 6. Quality of the Evaluation

**Score: 2 (fair).** Real strengths — same-model control, seven baselines, ablations, CIs and exact tests, candid limitations — are undermined by: a structurally one-sided headline test with the decisive control absent (W1); untested and non-significant "best on both axes" claims (W2); a self-favoring primary metric whose robustness defense contradicts the paper's own table (W3); cost figures that are approximations presented as exact, over an emulated endpoint with unknown scaffolding (W4); a cached baseline that violates provider minimum-prefix rules (W5); and pooled inference over 5 heavily heterogeneous clusters with n = 40 per assignment and 12% of the available corpus used (W7).

## 7. Reproducibility

**Score: 3 (good).** The artifact plan is strong: harness, prompts, synthesized repairers, all raw prompt/response strings (explicitly enabling exact re-tokenization), seeded scripted splits from a public corpus, deterministic local search, and per-round builder transcripts. What blocks a 4: model identity is only "Sonnet/Haiku tier, July 2026 snapshots," decoding parameters are unstated (contradicting the checklist's claim), and the LLM side ran through a bespoke agent-harness endpoint that third parties cannot reconstruct — so the analysis is reproducible, but the LLM measurements themselves are not independently re-obtainable.

## 8. Ethical Considerations

No blocking concerns. Student programs come from a publicly released, de-identified corpus; no new human-subjects data. The Ethical Statement sensibly limits scope to feedback under instructor oversight. Two suggestions: (1) the fallback path transmits student code to a commercial API — data-protection implications (e.g., FERPA/GDPR-style constraints on student work) deserve a sentence, and are actually an underused argument *for* the local-repair paradigm; (2) validated-but-test-overfitting patches shown as "correct" feedback could mislead students; the paper acknowledges suite-overfitting under construct validity but should connect it to the pedagogy risk.

## 9. Overall Evaluation

**Score: 5 (marginally below the acceptance threshold).**

The paper is well written, the amortization idea is attractive, the arithmetic is impressively self-consistent, and the safety-by-construction engineering is sound. But for a paper whose thesis is an efficiency claim, too much of the evidentiary core is soft: the only significant quality comparison is guaranteed in direction by construction and its natural confound (validated second attempts) is never controlled; the primary metric is bespoke, threshold-unjustified, and its robustness defense is contradicted by the paper's own Table 1; the costs are uncalibrated character-count estimates from an emulated endpoint presented as "exact token-level accounting"; one baseline (cached) is unimplementable under the cited providers' actual caching rules; and generalization rests on five contaminated, heavily clustered assignments analyzed as 200 iid samples. Convincing answers to Q1-Q5 (especially the equal-budget control and exact re-tokenization) could move me to a 6; as submitted, the cost-structure argument is credible but the quality-superiority and "dominant configuration" claims are not yet supported at the standard of rigor the paper itself advertises.

**Confidence: 4 (confident).** I verified the paper's internal arithmetic, cost model, and statistical claims in detail; I did not run the artifacts.
