# AAAI-27 Review — "Use LLMs to Build Repair Tools, Not Patch Generation: Cost-Effective Automated Program Repair"

**Reviewer:** R1 (APR / SE4AI / LLM-systems)

---

## 1. Summary

The paper targets automated repair of buggy student submissions to programming assignments and argues that the dominant deployment paradigm — one LLM query per submission — is economically mismatched with a "many-instances, one-specification" regime, since ~79% of each repair prompt is byte-identical across a class. The proposed system, CoEP, spends the LLM once per assignment: a builder agent reads the handout, test suite, 3 reference solutions, and 5 sample bugs, and synthesizes an executable symbolic repairer (problem-specific AST rewrite rules over a generic bounded edit search), refined for up to 3 harness-driven rounds; every patch is independently re-validated (test pass + relative-patch-size cap τ=0.35), and unrepaired submissions escalate to conventional per-submission LLM repair. On 5 assignments from the public Refactory corpus (1,685 real CS1 Python submissions; 200 held out for evaluation), the synthesized repairers alone fix 47.5% locally with zero unsafe outputs; the hybrid reaches 90.0–90.5% quality repairs versus 86.0% for the same-model per-submission baseline (exact McNemar p=0.008), and matches an enforced-retry baseline exactly (98.5%) at 1.8× lower marginal cost. The build cost of $0.256/assignment breaks even within ~154–295 submissions, inside a single course offering for this corpus. Eight baselines (plain/cached/few-shot/enforced-retry per-submission repair, cheap model, FrugalGPT-style cascade, generic-search-only, and the non-LLM Refactory tool) plus ablations of rules, refinement, and builder capability are reported under one validation harness and cost convention.

## 2. Strengths and Weaknesses

### Strengths

**S1 — A crisp, well-motivated deployment observation, made concrete.** The cost anatomy (Sec. "Cost Anatomy") is genuinely informative: the 439-token repair prompt decomposes into 56% preamble / 21% student code / 23% instructions, only the 55% contiguous prefix is cacheable in principle, and — a nice concrete detail — the whole prompt sits below the provider's 1,024-token cache minimum, so provider caching cannot engage at all on the plain prompt. The framing "caching serves the redundancy at the wrong granularity" is the paper's best conceptual sentence, and the paradigm proposal (amortize specification-level reasoning into a per-assignment executable artifact) follows naturally.

**S2 — Unusually honest and complete baseline matrix, including the arm that erases the headline gap.** The paper does not hide the comparison that most weakens its quality story: giving the per-submission baseline CoEP's own enforcement (validator + one minimality-feedback retry) reaches 98.5% quality — above every CoEP configuration — and CoEP+retry merely reproduces the identical 197/200 set at lower cost (discordant 0:0). Reporting this, plus the n.s. cascade comparison (discordant 4:0, p=0.125, correctly labeled n.s.), plus the structural one-sidedness of hybrid-vs-baseline McNemar tests, is evaluation candor well above the norm for this literature.

**S3 — Internal numeric consistency is excellent.** I re-derived the headline numbers and they all check out: $2.73×10⁻³/sub from 439/94 tokens at $3/$15 per Mtok; the $2.10 cache bound and 23% saving; build $1.28 total / $0.256 per assignment from 51,227+75,015 tokens (= "≈94 baseline repairs"); break-evens 197 / 154 / 295 / 173; the Haiku-builder upgrade break-even ≈230 (=(0.256−0.022)/(0.375·2.73×10⁻³)); McNemar p=0.008 from discordant 0:8 and p<10⁻²⁴ from 0:82; the "86% of everything the local path repairs" (82/95); all four Wilson intervals in Table 1 exactly; and the generic-hybrid row ($2.55×10⁻³, 86.0%) exactly. This level of verifiability is rare and raises my trust in the artifact.

**S4 — Safety-by-construction is real and correctly exploited.** The local path emitted zero test-failing and zero over-cap patches on 200 attempts — abstention is the only failure mode — because the repairer's outputs are independently re-validated and it is "contractually forbidden" from self-reporting success. For an education deployment this abstain-or-correct property matters, and it is what makes the "fronting any protocol preserves its quality set" composition argument sound.

**S5 — The ablations answer the right questions.** Generic-search-only collapsing to 6.5% (13/200) vs CoEP-local's 47.5% cleanly isolates the synthesized rules as the source of power; the refinement analysis is specific and credible (ast.unparse whole-file reformatting blowing the RPS cap on every candidate, fixed in round 2 by surgical line edits: 0/5→5/5); the Haiku-as-builder rerun (10.0%, 14/25 build success) shows synthesis is a frontier-capability task while per-assignment amortization keeps that affordable. These are the paradigm-relevant ablations, not filler.

**S6 — Claims are mostly scoped honestly.** The paper states explicitly that hybrid cost remains linear in enrollment ("a smaller slope plus a one-off intercept, not zero marginal cost"), flags the 25–85% per-assignment heterogeneity, names the low-traffic boundary case (top_k, 108 submissions), and runs the Refactory tool with an oracle cross-check (its own oracle 91.0% vs the harness's 90.5% test-pass) to show the 56.0% quality figure is a patch-size effect, not a port artifact.

### Weaknesses

**W1 — The quality "win" is enforcement, not paradigm — and the framing oversells it.** The abstract and intro foreground "exceeding the per-submission frontier baseline (86.0%; p=0.008)", but the paper's own Table 2 shows this gap is entirely the baseline's missing validator: with a size check + one retry, the per-submission paradigm hits 98.5%, and CoEP adds exactly zero quality on top (identical 197/200). The p=0.008 comparison (discordant 0:8) is structurally guaranteed to be one-directional and measures only "8 instances where one-shot generation overshot a cap it was never allowed to check." The real contribution is therefore cost/latency engineering at quality parity, which the paper eventually says plainly ("same quality at a smaller slope") — but two specific claims cross from framing into error: (a) the RQ1 box asserts the hybrid "exceeds every per-submission configuration," contradicted by the retry arm (98.5%, a per-submission protocol) in Table 2; (b) "CoEP+cascade … at the study's lowest marginal cost" (intro and RQ2 text) is contradicted by the study's own Table 2, where cheap-model (Haiku) is $0.90×10⁻³ < $1.07×10⁻³. Both need qualification ("lowest among quality-competitive arms"; "every unenforced single-shot configuration").

**W2 — The absolute economic stakes are never confronted.** At $2.73×10⁻³ per submission, one assignment-offering of 337 buggy submissions costs ≈$0.92 on the baseline; CoEP saves ≈$0.44 per assignment per term (≈$2 per course). Even at MOOC scale (10⁴ submissions/assignment), the saving is ≈$13/assignment. The intro's rhetoric ("cost grows linearly with enrollment and recurs every term") is structurally true but the constants are tiny; the strongest practical arguments are actually the operational ones (sub-second local latency, no rate limits, no provider dependence at deadline spikes, auditability) — and none of these are measured (no throughput/latency-under-load experiment, no rate-limit incident analysis). A cost-motivated paper should either scale the dollars honestly (platform serving thousands of courses; richer multi-attempt protocols where per-bug cost is 10–100×) or elevate the measured operational properties to first-class claims.

**W3 — The evaluation slice is smaller than it needed to be, and the headline test is thin.** Only 200 of 1,685 available submissions (40/assignment) are evaluated. The "eight arms tractable" justification applies to LLM arms only; CoEP-local, generic-only, and Refactory are zero-marginal-cost and could have run on all 1,685, which would have tightened the paper's most load-bearing number (47.5% local coverage; Wilson ±6.9pp — the setup's "±5pp" claim is optimistic at mid-range rates) and the per-assignment heterogeneity essentially for free. The 90.0-vs-86.0 result rests on 8 discordant instances clustered within ≤5 assignments; "the direction holds in all five assignments, +0 to +4" concedes at least one assignment contributes zero (a tie is not a direction), and with 5 clusters no cluster-robust test is possible. The conclusions most likely to survive scaling are the cost ones, not the quality ordering.

**W4 — No sensitivity analysis for the constants that define the primary metric and the method.** τ=0.35 is the fulcrum of the entire quality story (the baseline's 14% cap-busts are the headline gap; Refactory's 90.5%→56.0% collapse is a τ effect), yet no τ-sweep is reported — at τ=0.5 the baseline plausibly matches or beats the hybrid, and at τ=0.25 the ordering could shift again given the frontier's smaller accepted patches (0.093 vs 0.115 on shared instances). Refactory and PyDex use RPS as a *metric*, not a 0.35 *cap*, so the threshold is this paper's own construct and needs a robustness curve. Likewise k=5 build bugs and R_max=3 are untested knobs; the 25% local rate on remove_extras is attributed to "what five sample bugs can teach," which is exactly the hypothesis a k-sweep would test, and the framework's own economics could price it.

**W5 — External validity and a residual contamination asymmetry.** Five tiny CS1 assignments (~92-token programs), one course, one language, one model family. The symmetric-contamination argument is well made for per-submission arms (same model everywhere; patches must survive execution), but it does not fully cover the builder: the builder is shown 5 bugs yet may have memorized the *corpus-wide bug distribution* of this exact public dataset (circulating since 2019), inflating the 47.5% local coverage beyond what a fresh assignment would see. The Haiku-collapse counterargument (same putative memorization, 10% coverage) is suggestive but confounds memorization with synthesis capability. One private or post-cutoff assignment would have settled this; none is attempted.

**W6 — The amortization design space is sampled at only two points.** The paper compares "amortize into an executable rule module" against "pay per query," but skips natural middle points that share the many-instances structure: (i) amortize into a *textual* per-assignment repair guide (misconception list + fix recipes) prepended to a cheap model's per-submission prompt — plausibly lifting the Haiku arm well above 65% at ~$1×10⁻³ with no symbolic machinery; (ii) patch retrieval / semantic caching over previously repaired submissions; (iii) per-assignment distillation. The parenthetical dismissal of patch caching ("a patch is specific to one buggy program") is asserted, not measured. Without (i) especially, "build a symbolic tool" vs "build a prompt" is unresolved, and (i) is cheaper to build than CoEP.

**W7 — Minor but real presentation defects.** Mean local time is 0.8s in Deployment (macro) but 0.9s in Table 1 and RQ1 text; the saving factor is "1.9–2.5×" in the intro but "1.8–2.5×" in the RQ2 box and conclusion; Discussion claims ratios/break-evens are "unaffected by calibration error" while Protocol correctly concedes ±20% differential tokenizer error shifts break-evens by the same factor; Table 2's cascade row ($1.94×10⁻³) is ~4% above what the stated averages reconstruct ($1.86×10⁻³ = 0.90 + 0.35·2.73), presumably per-instance variation, but unexplained.

## 3. Questions for the Authors

1. **τ-sensitivity (most decision-relevant).** Report quality repair rate for the frontier baseline, CoEP-local, CoEP+frontier, retry, and Refactory at τ ∈ {0.25, 0.30, 0.40, 0.50}. Does the hybrid-vs-baseline ordering (90.0 vs 86.0) and the Refactory gap survive away from 0.35?
2. **Full-corpus local evaluation.** CoEP-local, generic-only, and Refactory are free to run. What are their rates on all 1,685 submissions, and does 47.5% (and the 25–85% spread) hold?
3. **Amortize-into-prompt baseline.** If the builder instead writes a per-assignment textual repair guide consumed by the Haiku-tier model per submission, what quality/cost does that achieve? This is the cheapest competitor exploiting the same structure and is currently absent.
4. **Scoping of two claims.** Please reconcile (a) RQ1's "exceeds every per-submission configuration" with the retry arm's 98.5%, and (b) "the study's lowest marginal cost" for CoEP+cascade ($1.07×10⁻³) with the cheap-model arm ($0.90×10⁻³) in Table 2.
5. **Discordant-pair geography.** For the 0:8 (hybrid vs baseline) and 0:9 (cascade-fb vs baseline, p=0.004) results, give the per-assignment breakdown. How many assignments contribute 0?
6. **Build-set size.** At k=10 or k=20 build bugs (still trivially cheap), does remove_extras' 25% local rate improve, and what is the marginal build cost per point of coverage?
7. **Contamination probe.** Can you run the builder on one assignment authored after the models' cutoffs (or a private course assignment) and report local coverage, even as a case study?
8. **Decoding and snapshots.** What temperature/top-p were used for all arms, and what exact model snapshot IDs? "Claude Sonnet/Haiku tier, July 2026" is not pinned enough to rerun.

## 4. Significance (1–4): **3**

The many-instances/one-specification framing is timely and the "amortize LLM reasoning into a verifiable executable artifact" recipe will interest the SE4AI and agents communities beyond this niche; the head-to-head against the classical tool and the enforcement-parity result (a validator+retry closes most of the quality gap for free) are findings of independent value. Significance is capped by the small absolute dollar stakes (W2), the 47.5% local coverage (the paradigm halves queries rather than eliminating them), and the CS1-scale scope; the operational benefits that would raise the stakes are asserted, not measured.

## 5. Justification of the Approach (1–4): **3**

The design is well reasoned: independent re-validation with abstention-only failure, a cap that operationalizes a real pedagogical requirement, refinement driven by harness outcomes, and a fallback that guarantees no regression relative to the protocol it fronts. The composition argument (fronting preserves the fallback's quality set) is sound given S4 and is verified empirically (identical 197/200). Held back from a 4 by unexamined constants (τ, k, R_max — W4) and by the unexplored middle of the amortization design space (W6), which leaves "synthesize a symbolic tool" only partially justified against cheaper amortization forms.

## 6. Quality of the Evaluation (1–4): **3**

Same-model paradigm control, eight baselines with uniform validation and cost accounting, correct paired statistics with disclosed discordant counts, per-assignment directions, and honest negative results put this above the bar of typical APR evaluations, including the AAAI-25 assignment-repair papers this should be judged against. It falls short of a 4 for concrete reasons: a 200/1,685 slice with no cost excuse for the free arms (W3), a primary-metric threshold with no sensitivity analysis (W4), a headline significance test carried by 8 clustered discordant instances, one model family, and two verifiable overclaims against its own tables (W1).

## 7. Reproducibility (1–4): **3**

Strong on paper: seeded scripted splits from a public corpus, deterministic local search, released prompts/repairers/raw LLM strings/harness/analysis scripts, closed-form break-even, and a detailed checklist; my independent re-derivation of ~15 reported quantities succeeded (S3). Not a 4 because decoding parameters are unstated, model versions are identified only as "July 2026 snapshots" of a tier, the tokenizer is an approximation (chars/3.6) with provider tokenizers unavailable, and API-side stochasticity of the builder means the central artifact (the five repairers) is reproducible only as released, not re-derivable.

## 8. Ethical Considerations

No significant concerns. The corpus is the publicly released, de-identified Refactory dataset; no new human-subjects data. The system produces feedback, not grades, under stated instructor oversight, and the abstain-or-correct property plus the minimality cap mitigate the main harm modes (wrong feedback; solution-revealing rewrites). Two soft notes the camera-ready could add: (1) automatically *repairing* student code, even minimally, can shortcut learning if surfaced as patches rather than hints — a sentence on intended presentation to students would help; (2) the per-assignment repairer encodes a misconception model of the class and should be treated as instructor-facing material, not shipped to students.

## 9. Overall Evaluation: **7/10** (accept) — Confidence: **4**

This is an honest, internally consistent, carefully controlled systems paper built around one good idea whose evidence now matches its (mostly) calibrated claims: quality parity with every per-submission protocol at a 1.8–2.5× smaller cost slope, break-even inside one offering, zero unsafe local outputs, and ablations that correctly locate the power in synthesized rules. The remaining weaknesses — enforcement-vs-paradigm framing with two fixable overclaims, unexamined τ/k sensitivity, a smaller-than-necessary evaluation slice, small absolute stakes, and a missing amortize-into-prompt baseline — are real and keep it from 8, but they are robustness and scoping gaps on top of a sound core rather than threats to its central result. Relative to recently accepted AAAI APR/education papers, the evaluation discipline (same-model control, enforcement-parity arm, disclosed discordants, cost accounting) is above par while scale is at par. Accept, with the τ-sweep, full-corpus local numbers, and claim-scoping fixes (Q1, Q2, Q4) expected in the camera-ready.
