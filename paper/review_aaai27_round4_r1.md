# AAAI-27 Review — "Use LLMs to Build Repair Tools, Not Patch Generation: Cost-Effective Automated Program Repair"

Reviewer: R1 (APR / SE4AI / LLM-systems). Round 4.

---

## 1. Summary

The paper targets automated repair of buggy student submissions to programming assignments and argues that the dominant deployment paradigm — one LLM query per submission — is economically mismatched with the setting, since 77% of each prompt is byte-identical across all submissions to the same assignment. It proposes CoEP: an LLM "builder" agent reads the assignment specification, tests, three reference solutions, and five sample bugs once, and synthesizes an executable per-assignment symbolic repairer (problem-specific AST rewrite rules over a generic bounded edit search), refined through at most three validation-feedback rounds; every emitted patch is independently re-validated (test pass plus a relative-patch-size cap of 0.35), and unrepaired submissions escalate to conventional per-submission LLM repair. On 5 assignments and 1,685 real CS1 Python submissions from the public Refactory dataset, with the same frontier model powering all LLM arms, the synthesized repairers alone fix 47.5% of 200 held-out submissions at zero marginal LLM cost (50.7% on the full 1,660-submission corpus), and the hybrid reaches 90.0–90.5% "quality repair rate" (test-pass within the size cap) versus 86.0% for the per-submission baseline (McNemar p=0.008). The build cost of $0.256 per assignment breaks even after roughly 154–295 submissions, and fronting any per-submission protocol (plain, cascade, validated-retry) preserves that protocol's quality at a 1.8–2.5x smaller marginal cost — including exactly matching the strongest enforced-retry protocol (98.5%, identical 197/200 set) at 1.8x lower cost. Ablations show the LLM-synthesized rules, not the generic search (6.5%), carry the local path, and that a cheap-model builder collapses to 10.0%.

## 2. Strengths and Weaknesses

### Strengths

**S1. A crisp, well-motivated reframing with an explicit cost model.** The many-instances/one-specification observation is developed quantitatively, not rhetorically: the prompt anatomy (241/99/99 tokens; 77% invariant, only 55% in a contiguous cacheable prefix; the 439-token prompt below the 1,024-token provider caching minimum) is concrete and correct, and the break-even formalization N* = C/Δ with measured C and Δ is exactly the right decision-theoretic object for an instructor. The paper also scopes its own claim honestly: hybrid cost remains linear ("a 1.8–2.5x smaller slope plus an intercept, not zero marginal cost"), the build decision is per assignment, and the lowest-traffic assignment (top_k, 108 submissions) is explicitly named as the boundary of the regime.

**S2. Unusual methodological discipline for this literature.** The same frontier model powers every LLM arm including the builder, so paradigm and model are not confounded; one validation harness and one metric are applied to every arm including the classical tool; costs are computed under one stated convention from raw strings; repair rates carry Wilson CIs; paired claims use exact McNemar with per-assignment direction reporting; and — rarely seen — the authors build the strongest version of their opponent (enforcement-parity "validator + 1 retry" baseline, which at 98.5% beats their own hybrid) and report it prominently rather than burying it.

**S3. The numbers reconcile under adversarial checking.** I verified the arithmetic: base cost $2.73e-3 = 439x$3/M + 94x$15/M; cached $2.10e-3; build $1.28 = 51,227x$3/M + 75,015x$15/M, i.e. $0.256/assignment ≈ 94 baseline repairs; break-evens 197 (frontier), 154 (cascade fallback), 294 (vs cascade), 173 (retry); ratios 1.81–2.55x matching "1.8–2.5x"; p=0.008 from discordant 0:8, p=0.004 from 9:0, p≈4.1e-25 from 0:82; 82/95 = 86% rules share; builder-upgrade break-even ≈229; all four table CIs ([85.1,93.4], [40.7,54.4], [3.8,10.8], [48.3,53.1]) are exact Wilson intervals for the stated counts. Internal consistency of this quality is itself evidence of care.

**S4. The ablations genuinely answer the sources-of-power question.** Generic-only at 6.5% (13/200, discordant 0:82) establishes that the synthesized problem-specific rules are essentially the whole local path; the refinement-round forensics are concrete and mechanistic (ast.unparse reformatting blowing the cap 0/5 -> 5/5; round-3 rescue of top_k), showing refinement fixes delivery defects, not comprehension; the Haiku-builder arm (10.0%, 14/25 build success) cleanly separates builder capability from paradigm.

**S5. The safety property and the exact-inheritance result are the paper's strongest findings.** The local path emitted zero test-failing and zero over-cap patches across all attempts — abstention is the only failure mode — which is the right design for a system whose output is feedback to a learner. Its consequence is empirically confirmed to be exact: CoEP+retry repairs the identical 197/200 set as the retry protocol (discordant 0:0, per-assignment identical) at $1.78e-3 vs $3.26e-3. "Same quality set, smaller slope, verified instance-by-instance" is a clean, defensible contribution independent of any metric dispute.

**S6. Fair treatment of the classical competitor.** Refactory runs in its strongest published configuration with all 2,442 correct student solutions (vastly more per-assignment data than CoEP's 3+5), and the authors cross-check their harness against the tool's own oracle (91.0% vs their 90.5% test-pass), isolating the quality gap to patch size rather than re-validation artifacts.

### Weaknesses

**W1. The headline "exceeds the per-submission baseline" is a parity artifact, and the paper's own ceiling arm proves it.** On the field-standard metric (test-pass), every LLM arm is at 100.0% — indistinguishable. The 90.0/90.5 vs 86.0 win exists only under the authors' hard RPS<=0.35 acceptance gate, and only against a baseline denied the validator that CoEP has by construction: granting the baseline the same enforcement (validator + one retry) lifts it to 98.5%, strictly above every CoEP-with-plain-fallback configuration. The genuinely supportable claim is therefore "identical quality at 1.8x lower marginal cost" (S5), not "higher quality than the deployed paradigm" — yet the abstract and introduction lead with the latter (p=0.008). The paper is transparent about all of this (RQ2 names retry the "quality ceiling"), but the framing inflates a cost result into a quality result. Relatedly, τ=0.35 is cited to Refactory/PyDex, but those systems report patch size as a statistic; using it as a hard acceptance gate — the thing the entire headline depends on — is this paper's construct (the τ-sweep mitigates but cannot dissolve the choice of primary metric).

**W2. The absolute economic stakes, as measured, are cents.** At the paper's own numbers, an assignment's entire per-submission frontier repair bill for one offering is 337 x $2.73e-3 ≈ $0.92; CoEP's net saving vs. the frontier-fallback configuration is ≈ $0.18 per assignment per term (a few dollars per course even at MOOC 10x). The break-even algebra is correct but the practical significance of the dollar claim on this corpus is negligible; the case for mattering rests on extrapolation (richer prompts at $6.5e-3, retry at $3.26e-3, many assignments, many terms) and on the non-cost properties (latency <1s, no rate limits, deadline-spike robustness, auditability) — which are asserted but not stress-tested (no deadline-spike or rate-limit experiment). Falling model prices erode the savings side; the title's central promise is the paper's weakest quantitative leg.

**W3. The most natural competitor hybrid is missing: classical tool + same fallback.** Refactory standalone quality-repairs 56.0% locally at zero LLM cost, and its failures are locally detectable under the authors' own harness (tests + RPS are computable without a model). A Refactory-fronted hybrid would, by the paper's own arithmetic, escalate ~44% of traffic at ≈$1.20e-3/submission with zero build cost and plausibly land near ~91% quality — i.e., in the neighborhood of CoEP+frontier (90.0% at $1.43e-3) while beating it on cost. The paper's answer would be data requirements (2,442 correct solutions vs. 3 refs + 5 bugs) and per-instance latency (5.5s), but that data-efficiency advantage is never isolated either (no Refactory-with-3+5-programs arm). As it stands, the claim that LLM synthesis is the right way to build the amortized local layer is under-supported: a 2019 non-LLM tool fronting the same fallback may already realize most of the cost structure.

**W4. A second missing cell: enforcement applied to the cheap arms.** The paper demonstrates that enforcement is the single most powerful lever in the study (86.0 -> 98.5 on the frontier model), but never applies it to the cheap model or inside the cascade. Haiku + validator + retry(s) at ~$1.8–2.7e-3, or a cascade in which Haiku self-retries before escalating, are zero-build-cost configurations that could plausibly crowd the Pareto frontier that CoEP+cascade ($1.07e-3 at 90.5%) is claimed to own ("lowest marginal cost of any quality-competitive arm" is true only over the arms tested).

**W5. External validity is thin for a paradigm claim: 5 assignments, one course, one language, one model family — and one build trajectory each.** The core artifact, the synthesized repairer, is sampled exactly once per assignment per builder tier (5 frontier + 5 Haiku syntheses total); the 47.5% local coverage, the 3-of-5 refinement rescues, and the 27.9–71.4% per-assignment dispersion all sit on n=1 stochastic draws. The authors concede "each assignment reflects one stochastic builder trajectory" and defer multi-seed replication to the released harness, but for a paper whose contribution is the synthesis pipeline, variance over the pipeline is the first thing to quantify. Pooled McNemar tests at the submission level (n=200) additionally understate clustering within 5 assignments; per-assignment direction reporting helps but 5 clusters is few.

**W6. Contamination is argued away only for the paradigm delta, not for the absolute numbers or generality.** The Refactory corpus (including corrected versions of these very submissions) has been public since 2019; the symmetry argument ("same model powers every arm") is sound for baseline-vs-CoEP comparisons, and the ast.unparse/Haiku corroborations are clever, but the builder's "problem-specific rules" for canonical NUS CS1 problems may still encode memorized fix patterns, inflating the 47.5% local coverage and the implied generalization to novel assignments. A small post-cutoff or private-assignment control is absent and would be decisive.

**W7. Visible-suite overfitting differentially favors the proposed system, by construction.** CoEP's local search returns the first candidate passing the 5–11 visible tests — it optimizes the evaluation oracle directly — while the LLM arms generate without test access. The RPS cap blocks wholesale replacement but not small test-overfitted edits that pass 5–11 I/O checks without fixing the misconception. The authors name this ("suite overfitting favors it differentially; hidden-test evaluation is the natural next control") but ship neither a hidden-test split nor a manual semantic audit of even a sample of local patches; for a pedagogy-motivated system this is a real gap between "passes the suite" and "correct feedback."

**W8. Cost-accounting opacities.** (a) Composed-arm costs do not back out from the stated averages: cascade $1.94e-3 vs. naive 0.90 + 0.35x2.73 = $1.86e-3; CoEP+cascade $1.07e-3 vs. 0.525x1.94 = $1.02e-3; CoEP+retry $1.78e-3 vs. $1.71e-3. The direction (harder escalated subsets cost more) is plausible and even corroborates genuine measurement, but no decomposition is given. (b) All dollars are modeled from a chars/3.6 tokenizer approximation and list prices, not billed usage (disclosed; raw strings released). (c) The "±5pp Wilson intervals" justification for n=40/assignment is optimistic at mid-range rates (±7pp at 47.5%). (d) Cascade quality (88.5%) implies the frontier repairs only ~67% of Haiku-escalated instances vs. 86% overall — worth one sentence of explanation (adverse selection), currently absent.

## 3. Questions for the Authors

1. **Classical-hybrid control (W3):** Run Refactory + frontier fallback under your exact accounting. What quality/$ does it achieve, and what does Refactory achieve when restricted to CoEP's data budget (3 references + 5 sample bugs)? This is the pivotal comparison for whether LLM synthesis, rather than any amortized local front-end, is the contribution.
2. **Cheap enforcement (W4):** What do Haiku+validator+1-retry, and a cascade with Haiku self-retry before escalation, score on the Table 2 ladder?
3. **Hidden-test / semantic audit (W7):** Can you hold out a subset of instructor tests (or hand-audit ~50 local patches) and report the semantic-correctness rate of locally repaired submissions vs. fallback repairs?
4. **Fresh-assignment control (W6):** Report builder results on 2–3 assignments provably outside training data (post-cutoff or private). Does local coverage stay near ~50%, and do the rules still target observed misconceptions rather than canonical fixes?
5. **Synthesis variance (W5):** For at least two assignments, re-run synthesis with 3–5 seeds. What is the spread of local coverage and of rounds-to-convergence?
6. **Composed-cost decomposition (W8a):** Provide the per-subset token/cost breakdown explaining $1.94e-3, $1.07e-3, $1.78e-3 vs. the naive products of stated averages.
7. **Deployment claims (W2):** For the deadline-spike/latency/rate-limit argument, can you report any measured throughput comparison (e.g., 500 submissions in one hour, local vs. API), or is this argued only from first principles?
8. **Metric provenance (W1):** PyDex and Refactory report RPS distributions; which prior work, if any, uses RPS<=0.35 as a hard acceptance criterion? If none, please state plainly that the primary metric is introduced here, and report full RPS distributions per arm (beyond the τ-sweep).

## 4. Significance — 3/4

The deployment-economics reframing is genuinely useful and transferable (the closing recipe — amortize LLM reasoning into verifiable executable artifacts wherever many instances share a specification — is a real idea, adjacent to LLM-as-tool-maker but with the verification-and-quality-cap twist that education forces). The exact-inheritance result (identical 197/200 at 1.8x lower cost) is a clean systems contribution. Significance is capped by W1–W3: on the corpus studied the quality story reduces to a cost story, the measured dollars are cents per course, and it remains unshown that the amortized layer must be LLM-synthesized rather than a classical tool fronting the same fallback. Relative to AAAI-25's counterexample-guided repair and AAAI-26's retrieval-guided repair — both algorithmic advances inside the per-submission paradigm — this is a complementary, systems-level contribution of comparable but not greater import.

## 5. Justification of the Approach — 3/4

The design is well-reasoned and internally coherent: never-trust-self-reports independent validation, abstention-only failure, size caps as pedagogical constraint, escalation so coverage is never sacrificed, and a deliberately austere single-shot refinement loop with measured build cost. The formulation (repairer ρ_A: b -> p ∪ {⊥} plus hybrid) is clean. Held back from 4 because the central design commitment — that the local layer should be an LLM-synthesized rule module rather than (a) a classical per-assignment tool or (b) a cheap model under the same enforcement — is motivated but not experimentally justified against those alternatives (W3, W4), and because the primary quality gate is an authorial construct doing decisive rhetorical work (W1).

## 6. Quality of the Evaluation — 3/4

Within its scope this is one of the more careful evaluations I have reviewed in LLM-APR: same-model control, eight baselines including an enforcement-parity arm and a fairly-run classical tool with oracle cross-check, uniform harness and cost convention, exact paired statistics with per-assignment directions, full-corpus replication of the local arm, a τ-sweep, and ablations that isolate rules, refinement, and builder capability. All headline numbers I checked reconcile (S3). It does not earn a 4 because of the missing decisive arms (Refactory-hybrid, cheap+enforcement), the n=1 synthesis trajectories, 40-submission subsamples over only 5 clustered assignments, no hidden-test or human semantic assessment, and an unaddressed-by-experiment contamination risk on a 2019-public corpus.

## 7. Reproducibility — 3/4

Strong on artifacts: harness, import pipeline, prompts, synthesized repairers, every raw prompt/response for both builder tiers, seeds, timeouts, τ, tokenizer convention, and pricing snapshot are all stated and promised in the supplementary repository; local search is deterministic and CPU-only; the reproducibility checklist is substantive. Two structural limits keep it at 3: the pipeline depends on commercial July-2026 model snapshots that will drift or disappear (raw transcripts allow verification of this run but not regeneration), and dollar figures are modeled from an approximate tokenizer rather than billed usage (disclosed, with raw strings released for re-tokenization).

## 8. Ethical Considerations

No significant concerns. Data are public (Refactory corpus of anonymized student programs); no new human-subject data. The system produces feedback, not grades, under instructor oversight, and its abstention-only local failure mode plus mandatory re-validation is the right safety posture for learner-facing output. Two minor notes the camera-ready could address: (i) automatically repaired code shown to students can short-circuit learning if presented as a full solution — the RPS cap mitigates but deployment guidance would help; (ii) the escalation path means student code is still transmitted to a commercial API for ~half of submissions, which some institutions restrict; worth one sentence.

## 9. Overall Evaluation — 6/10 (marginal accept), Confidence — 4/5

A fresh, honest, and unusually well-audited systems paper whose true result — the same quality set as the strongest per-submission protocol, verified instance-by-instance, at a 1.8–2.5x smaller cost slope with a sub-dollar per-assignment build — is real and cleanly established. It is held below a clear accept by an over-claimed headline (a quality win that is an enforcement-parity artifact), cents-scale measured stakes, two missing baselines that could plausibly occupy its claimed Pareto position (classical-tool hybrid; cheap model under enforcement), single-trajectory synthesis, and a contamination/hidden-test story argued rather than tested. A rebuttal supplying Q1–Q3 (even partially) would move me to 7; absent that, this sits at the acceptance boundary — above the bar on execution and candor, at the bar on evidence for its central comparative claim.

**Confidence: 4** — I work in this area, verified the paper's arithmetic and statistics directly, and know the cited systems (Refactory, PyDex, ChatRepair, FrugalGPT, the AAAI-25/26 assignment-repair papers); I did not execute the released artifacts.
