# AAAI-27 Review — "Use LLMs to Build Repair Tools, Not Patch Generation: Cost-Effective Automated Program Repair"

Reviewer 2 (ML systems / cost-efficient inference; experimental rigor, cost accounting, statistics)

---

## 1. Summary

The paper argues that per-submission LLM repair of programming assignments is economically mismatched with the classroom setting, because each query re-transmits assignment-constant content (measured at 77% of input tokens, with a 55% contiguous cacheable prefix on a 439-token prompt that falls below provider caching minimums). It proposes CoEP: an LLM "builder" agent reads the assignment specification, tests, 3 reference solutions, and 5 historical buggy submissions once, and synthesizes a deterministic per-assignment Python repairer (problem-specific AST rewrite rules over a generic bounded edit search), refined through at most 3 validation-feedback rounds; at deployment the repairer fixes submissions locally at zero marginal LLM cost and abstains ("bot") otherwise, escalating abstentions to any conventional per-submission protocol. On 5 assignments from the public Refactory corpus (1,685 compiling buggy submissions; 200 held-out evaluation instances, 40 per assignment), with the same frontier model powering all LLM arms, the local repairers fix 47.5% of held-out instances (50.7% [48.3, 53.1] on the full corpus) with zero unsafe outputs, and the hybrid preserves each fallback protocol's quality (identically 197/200 for the strongest validated-retry protocol) at 1.8–1.9x lower marginal cost, with a $0.256/assignment build cost breaking even at roughly 154–295 submissions on corpus-average economics (per-assignment 38–704). Comparisons cover plain/few-shot/cached per-submission repair, an enforcement-parity validated-retry baseline, a cheap model and FrugalGPT-style cascade, a generic-search-only ablation, and the non-LLM Refactory tool, plus ablations of the synthesized rules, refinement rounds, and builder capability, a hidden-oracle overfitting audit, and one independent end-to-end replication of the synthesis pipeline.

## 2. Strengths and Weaknesses

### Strengths

**S1 — The paradigm claim is cleanly formulated and the experimental design isolates it.** All LLM arms (baselines and CoEP's builder) use the same frontier model under one validation harness, one metric definition, and one cost convention, so the comparison is paradigm-vs-paradigm rather than model-vs-model. The "many-instances, one-specification" framing, the hybrid-with-escalation guarantee (the local path can only abstain, never emit a test-failing or over-cap patch, because the deployment harness re-validates), and the closed-form break-even N* = C/Delta make the economic claim falsifiable and portable.

**S2 — The cost arithmetic checks out and is conservative toward the baselines.** I recomputed the main quantities: baseline per-query cost 439 x $3/M + 94 x $15/M = $2.73e-3 (matches Table 2); the cache "discount" 241 x $3/M x 0.9 = $0.65e-3 = 23.9% of $2.73e-3, giving the $2.10e-3 no-minimum bound the paper grants the baseline even though the 439-token prompt cannot cache in practice; CoEP+frontier marginal 0.525 x 2.73 = $1.43e-3; break-evens 0.256/(2.73-1.43)e-3 = 197, 0.256/(2.73-1.07)e-3 = 154, 0.256/(1.94-1.07)e-3 = 294, 0.256/(3.26-1.78)e-3 = 173; the Haiku-builder upgrade 0.234/1.02e-3 = 229 ("~230"); build cost 51,227 x 3 + 75,015 x 15 = $1.28 total = $0.256/assignment = 94 baseline repairs. The paper also correctly measures composite-arm dollars as per-instance sums rather than products of averages and explains why the cascade's measured $1.94e-3 exceeds the naive $1.86e-3 (adverse selection of escalated instances) — a subtlety most cost papers get wrong.

**S3 — The enforcement-parity comparison is the right control, and the load-bearing claims do not rest on p-values.** Rather than beating only the weak plain-prompt convention, the paper builds the baseline a validator-plus-one-retry protocol (98.5% at $3.26e-3), shows CoEP fronting it repairs the identical 197/200 (discordant 0:0, per-assignment identical) at $1.78e-3, and honestly reframes the headline as inheritance-at-a-smaller-slope rather than quality superiority. The structural argument (a local path that never emits an unsafe patch cannot degrade the fallback's quality set) is sound and is what actually carries the paper.

**S4 — Unusual statistical candor.** The paper itself flags that hybrid-vs-baseline McNemar tests are structurally one-sided; that submissions cluster in only five assignments; and that the 8-unique-win edge over the plain baseline (0:8, exact p = 2 x 0.5^8 = 0.0078 — verified) is "suggestive" at cluster level because the per-assignment sign test on 3-0-2 is not significant (p = 0.25). The full-corpus re-run of the local arm (50.7% [48.3, 53.1] over 1,660 = 1,685 - 25 non-build submissions; Wilson interval verified) validates the n=200 estimate (47.5% [40.7, 54.4]; verified), and the internal consistency is tight: the 8 unique wins + 87 shared quality repairs = 95 local repairs, exactly.

**S5 — The suite-overfitting audit is a real audit, not a gesture.** The hidden-oracle probe (60 seeded inputs/assignment vs. the reference) finds a genuine differential overfit against CoEP (92.6% vs. 97.1% probe agreement), reports it, and then deploys the probe as a third validator to close it (coverage 47.5 -> 44.0%, hybrid quality unchanged). The disclosed numbers reconcile precisely where I could check them: 95 x (1 - 0.926) = 7 = the coverage drop from 95 to 88 instances; baseline probe-oracle quality 0.86 x 0.971 = 83.5% exactly as reported.

**S6 — The ablations locate the power convincingly.** Generic search alone collapses to 6.5% (13/200) vs. 47.5%; discordant 0:82 gives p = 2 x 0.5^82 = 4.1e-25 (verified), and 82/95 = 86% of local repairs are attributable to synthesized rules. The Haiku-tier builder collapses to 10.0% under identical prompts and R_max, and the refinement analysis (15/25 -> 25/25, with a concrete delivery-defect example: ast.unparse reformatting blowing the cap) shows the validate-refine loop is necessary, not decorative.

**S7 — Reproducibility posture is strong**: raw prompt/response strings, synthesized repairers, seeds, import pipeline, and the ported tool configuration are promised as artifacts; the approximate tokenizer (chars/3.6) is disclosed with a +/-20% differential sensitivity bound on break-evens; the checklist is meaningfully filled out.

### Weaknesses

**W1 — With five clusters, no pooled superiority claim can be cluster-robustly significant, and the promised per-assignment reporting is incomplete.** The Metrics section commits to "per-assignment directions alongside every pooled test," but the 7:0 comparison vs. Refactory+frontier (p = 0.016) and the 9:0 comparison of CoEP+cascade vs. plain frontier (p = 0.004) are reported without per-assignment breakdowns; only the 0:8 test and the 4:0 cascade comparison get cluster-level treatment. Note also that a five-cluster sign test cannot reach p < 0.05 unless all five assignments are strictly concordant, so every submission-level p-value in the paper is, at cluster level, at best suggestive — the paper concedes this for one test but the abstract still leads with p = 0.008. This does not undermine the inheritance/cost claims (arithmetic on measured quantities), but all quality-superiority language should be uniformly downgraded.

**W2 — The deployed-probe "slope +2%" claim does not reconcile with the disclosed accounting.** Deploying the probe validator shifts 7 of 200 instances (95 -> 88 local) to the fallback. Under the frontier fallback ($2.73e-3 average, and no plausible per-query cost below ~$1.5e-3 given the 340-token fixed prompt content), 7 extra escalations add ~$0.05–0.10e-3 per submission, i.e., +4% to +7% on the $1.43e-3 slope, not +2%. The claim is arithmetically plausible only if "slope" refers to the cascade-fallback configuration with all 7 shifted instances resolved by the cheap tier (~+3%). Since this number is load-bearing for the "overfitting is closed at negligible price" robustness claim, it needs an explicit derivation (see Q1).

**W3 — A quantitative misstatement in RQ1.** The Refactory tool's test-passing patches "average 0.386 RPS ..., twice our cap": 0.386 is 1.10x the 0.35 cap, not twice. (2x the cap would be 0.70; 0.386 is coincidentally ~2x the tool's cap-compliant mean of 0.187, which may be the confusion.) Small, but it inflates the headline contrast with the symbolic tool and should be corrected.

**W4 — "Multi-seed" replication is two runs, and the evaluation split is a single seed.** The independent end-to-end replication (coverage 43.0% vs. 47.5%, hybrid 88.0% vs. 90.0%, slope $1.56 vs. $1.43e-3) is welcome and honestly shows the weaker draw, but n=2 builds yields a range, not a variance estimate; the "<=2pp and <=9%" stability claim is based on one resample. All arms share one evaluation split (seed 7) of 40/assignment; the 8-win edge (4% of instances) is within plausible split-to-split variation and no split-sensitivity check is offered. The LLM arms are never run on the full corpus, so full-corpus hybrid quality/cost are extrapolations anchored only by the local arm's re-run.

**W5 — The contamination-symmetry argument is asserted rather than established.** "Memorization inflates the baseline at least as much as the builder" is not obvious: the builder's rules are evaluated on 40 unseen submissions, so memorized corpus-level bug distributions (which misconceptions occur, in which assignments) would advantage the builder arm in a way per-submission repair of an in-context program does not require. The corroborations offered (refinement fixes formatting defects; the cheap builder collapses) are equally consistent with a capability story and do not discriminate memorization. A perturbation probe (variable renaming / statement reordering of build and eval submissions) would have been cheap and decisive; none is reported.

**W6 — The tau-sweep is exact only for single-shot arms.** For unconstrained generations, recomputing acceptance from stored patch sizes at other tau is exact, and the conservativeness note for CoEP at tau < 0.35 is correct. But the validated-retry arm's behavior is protocol-dependent on tau (the retry triggers on the tau = 0.35 validator), so its "dominates at every tau" curve is an approximation whose direction of error is not analyzed. The figure cannot be audited from the source alone.

**W7 — External validity and per-assignment economics.** Five CS1 assignments, one course, one language, one model family (two tiers); per-assignment local coverage spans 27.9–71.4%, and the disclosed per-assignment break-evens show the paradigm losing on top_k (N* ~ 704 vs. 108 submissions/offering) — the paper is honest about this, but it means the headline "breaks even inside one offering" holds for three of five assignments, and the aggregate economics lean on the two easiest, high-volume assignments. The per-assignment build costs underlying N* in {38, 88, 241, 368, 704} are not tabulated; back-solving implies a ~7x spread (~$0.07 to ~$0.5), which should be shown.

**W8 — Minor accounting/reporting gaps.** Exact model snapshot identifiers appear only as "tier + July 2026" in the text (deferred to released config); decoding temperature is unstated; the chars/3.6 tokenizer is applied to arms with different input/output and code/prose mixes, and the output-heavy build (75k of 126k tokens, priced 5x) is most exposed to differential tokenizer error — bounded at +/-20% but unverified pending re-tokenization; Table 1's mean RPS is computed over cap-compliant repairs only, which mildly flatters arms with more cap-busts (disclosed in the caption, but easy to misread).

## 3. Questions for the Authors

1. **Probe-validator slope.** Show the arithmetic behind "slope +2%" after deploying the hidden-input probe: which fallback (frontier at $1.43e-3 or cascade at $1.07e-3), and what were the measured per-instance fallback costs of the 7 newly escalated submissions? Under the frontier fallback I compute at least +4% (7 x ~$1.5e-3 / 200) and ~+6.6% at average escalation cost.
2. **Cluster-level reporting.** Provide per-assignment discordance for the 7:0 (vs. Refactory+frontier) and 9:0 (CoEP+cascade vs. plain frontier) McNemar tests, as the Metrics section promises, and state plainly whether any pooled quality comparison survives any cluster-level test.
3. **Fallback response provenance.** In the hybrid arms, are escalated instances answered by the stored generations of the corresponding standalone arm (deterministic reuse) or by fresh calls? If reuse, "the identical 197/200, discordant 0:0" is structural rather than empirical; please say so and estimate the variation expected under fresh sampling.
4. **"Twice our cap."** Correct or clarify: 0.386 RPS is 1.1x the 0.35 cap.
5. **Tau-sweep for the retry arm.** How is the validated-retry arm recomputed at tau != 0.35 given its retry trigger is defined by the tau = 0.35 validator, and in which direction is the approximation biased?
6. **Contamination probe.** Does builder and baseline performance persist under semantics-preserving perturbation (identifier renaming, reordering) of build and evaluation submissions?
7. **Per-assignment break-even inputs.** Report per-assignment build cost and per-assignment marginal saving used for N* in {38, 88, 241, 368, 704} (top_k's implied build cost appears to be ~2x the average).
8. **Snapshots and decoding.** Give exact model snapshot IDs and decoding parameters (temperature/top-p) for all arms in the paper body.

## 4. Significance — 3/4

The identified regime (many instances, one specification) is real and extends beyond assignment repair to rubric grading, hint generation, and any high-volume templated LLM service; the paper gives it a concrete, falsifiable economics (slope vs. intercept, per-assignment break-even) and shows provider caching is structurally unable to capture the redundancy at this prompt scale. The demonstrated scope, however, is five CS1 Python assignments from one course, and the quality-side contribution is inheritance rather than improvement, so the significance is solid-but-domain-bounded rather than field-changing.

## 5. Justification of the Approach — 4/4

The design decisions are individually motivated and collectively coherent: amortize exactly the prompt content shown to be redundant; make the synthesized artifact verifiable by construction (independent re-validation, never-trust-self-reports, size cap as a pedagogical constraint with citations); use a hybrid so coverage is never sacrificed; and justify the refinement loop with a defect analysis showing single-shot synthesis would ship 3 of 5 broken repairers. The alternatives a skeptic would propose — caching, few-shot enrichment, cheap models, cascades, retrieval of past patches, the classical symbolic tool, generic search without synthesis — are each instantiated and priced rather than argued away.

## 6. Quality of the Evaluation — 3/4

Within its scope this is close to exemplary: same-model control, enforcement-parity baseline, eight-plus arms under one harness, ablations that isolate the mechanism, a self-incriminating overfitting audit with a deployed mitigation, a full-corpus check of the central coverage estimate, one independent replication, and cost accounting that survived my recomputation at every point I could check (Wilson intervals, McNemar p-values, all break-evens, the cascade's adverse-selection premium, the probe-audit bookkeeping). It falls short of a 4 on breadth and inference: five clusters cap the achievable statistical strength of every superiority claim (conceded only partially in the presentation), two pooled tests lack the promised per-assignment breakdowns, replication is n=2 with a single evaluation split, and I found two numeric misstatements (the "+2% slope" and "twice our cap"), one of which touches a robustness claim.

## 7. Reproducibility — 4/4

Seeded, scripted dataset import from a public corpus; released prompts, synthesized repairers, raw model payloads for both builder tiers and every round; deterministic local search; fixed split seed, timeout, threshold, and pricing snapshot; a disclosed tokenizer approximation with raw strings released for exact re-tokenization; and a closed-form break-even that any reader can re-price. The only gaps are exact snapshot IDs and decoding parameters in the text, and the inherent non-reproducibility of the stochastic builder (mitigated by releasing all raw generations and one replication).

## 8. Ethical Considerations

No significant concerns. The system produces feedback, not grades, under instructor oversight; uncovered students receive the full-LLM path, so no student population is systematically underserved by the cost optimization; data are public and anonymized student submissions used within their established research purpose; synthesized repairers are auditable artifacts, which is an ethics-positive property relative to opaque per-query generation. The paper should perhaps note that per-assignment rules could encode incorrect "fixes" for unanticipated-but-valid student approaches, though the test-suite validation and escalation path bound this risk.

## 9. Overall Evaluation — 7/10 (Accept)

**Justification.** The paper makes one clear, decision-relevant claim — LLM reasoning for assignment repair can be amortized into a verifiable per-assignment artifact that preserves any fallback protocol's quality at a 1.8–1.9x smaller marginal slope, breaking even within one offering for most assignments — and supports it with a same-model, enforcement-parity, honestly-clustered evaluation whose arithmetic I verified extensively and found sound. The genuine weaknesses (five clusters, n=2 replication, single split, an unproven contamination-symmetry assertion, and two numeric slips including the probe-slope claim) temper enthusiasm but do not threaten the central result, which is structural and arithmetic rather than inferential. The candor of the limitations analysis is well above the norm and increases my trust in the artifact. Fixing W1–W3 in a revision is straightforward.

**Confidence: 4/5.** I recomputed the statistics and cost accounting in detail and know this literature and cost-modeling practice well; I could not inspect the figures' underlying data, the released artifacts, or the raw model outputs.
