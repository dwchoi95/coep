# AAAI-27 Review — "Use LLMs to Build Repair Tools, Not Patch Generation: Cost-Effective Automated Program Repair"

Reviewer: R2 (ML systems / cost-efficient inference; experimental rigor, cost accounting, statistics)

---

## 1. Summary

The paper targets automated repair of buggy student submissions to programming assignments and argues that the dominant deployment — one LLM query per submission — is economically mismatched with a "many instances, one specification" regime, since 77% of each prompt is byte-identical across a class. The proposed system, CoEP, spends the LLM once per assignment: a builder agent reads the specification, tests, 3 reference solutions, and 5 sample bugs, and synthesizes a deterministic Python repairer (problem-specific AST rewrite rules over a generic bounded edit search), refined through at most 3 harness-driven validation rounds; at deployment the repairer runs locally and any abstention escalates to a conventional per-submission LLM call. On 5 assignments from the public Refactory corpus (1,685 real buggy CS1 Python submissions; 200 held-out for the arm comparisons), the local repairers alone fix 47.5% of evaluation submissions (50.7% [48.3, 53.1] on the full corpus) with zero unsafe outputs, and the hybrid attains 90.0–90.5% quality repairs (test-pass within an RPS <= 0.35 patch-size cap) vs. 86.0% for the same-model per-submission baseline, at $1.43 vs. $2.73 (x10^-3) per submission. Against an enforcement-parity baseline (validator + one retry, 98.5% quality at $3.26x10^-3), fronting with CoEP reproduces the identical 197/200 quality set at $1.78x10^-3; the $0.256/assignment build cost breaks even after ~154–295 submissions on corpus-average economics (per-assignment 38–704). Ablations show the LLM-synthesized rules, not the generic search, carry the local path (6.5% generic-only floor; a Haiku-tier builder collapses to 10.0%).

## 2. Strengths and Weaknesses

### Strengths

**S1. The cost accounting is unusually careful, and it survives independent recomputation — essentially every number checks out.** I recomputed: baseline per-submission cost 439 x $3/M + 94 x $15/M = $2.727x10^-3 (Table 2: 2.73); build cost 51,227 x $3/M + 75,015 x $15/M = $1.279 total, $0.2558/assignment (claimed 0.256), = 93.8 baseline queries (claimed "~94"); the no-minimum caching bound (241-token prefix at 0.1x) = $2.076x10^-3 (claimed 2.10) and a ~23–24% saving (claimed 23); break-evens 0.256/(2.73−1.43)e-3 = 196.9 (claimed 197), 0.256/(2.73−1.07)e-3 = 154.2 (claimed 154), 0.256/(1.94−1.07)e-3 = 294.3 (claimed ~295), 0.256/(3.26−1.78)e-3 = 173.0 (claimed ~173); the Haiku-to-frontier builder upgrade break-even (0.256−0.022)/((0.475−0.10) x 2.73e-3) = 228.6 (claimed ~230). All four headline Wilson intervals ([80.5, 90.1], [40.7, 54.4], [85.1, 93.4], [48.3, 53.1]) reproduce exactly. This level of verifiable internal consistency is rare.

**S2. The comparison design is the right one, and the paper builds its own strongest counter-arguments.** The same frontier model powers every LLM arm (paradigm, not model, is the treatment); the enforcement-parity arm (validator + 1 retry) deliberately lifts the per-submission baseline to 98.5% — above every CoEP-without-retry configuration — and the paper then rests its load-bearing claim on the symmetric comparison: CoEP+retry reproduces the *identical* 197/200 quality set (discordant 0:0, per-assignment identical) at 1.83x lower marginal cost. That claim is immune to the metric and clustering concerns below. The adverse-selection accounting (composite-arm dollars as per-instance sums, not products of averages; cascade $1.94 measured vs. $1.86 naive, with the mechanism quantified: the frontier repairs 67% of Haiku-escalated instances vs. 86% overall — 47/70, checks out) is exactly what a cost paper should do and almost none do.

**S3. Cross-table internal consistency is airtight.** Local quality repairs 95/200; 87 shared with the baseline's 172 implies 8 unique local wins — matching the reported 0:8 discordance and the hybrid's 180/200 = 95 + 85 (fallback quality on escalated instances 85/105 = 81% < 86%, consistent with adverse selection). McNemar p-values all reproduce: 0:8 -> 0.0078; 0:82 -> 4.14x10^-25 (matches the 4.1e-25 macro); 7:0 -> 0.0156; 9:0 -> 0.0039; 4:0 -> 0.125 (correctly reported n.s.). The 86%-of-local-repairs claim for synthesized rules is 82/95 = 86.3%. The Refactory-tool port is cross-checked against the tool's own oracle (91.0 vs. 90.5 test-pass).

**S4. Baseline breadth is genuinely strong for this literature.** Caching is handled honestly (the 439-token prompt is below the 1,024-token cacheable minimum, so the plain-prompt caching row is labeled a no-minimum bound; caching *is* applied where it engages, on the few-shot arm), few-shot gets information parity with the builder, the cascade gets a self-retry variant, the non-LLM tool runs in its strongest configuration with orders of magnitude more per-assignment data, and the retry protocol gives the baseline CoEP's own enforcement mechanism. The paper also volunteers unflattering facts: the frontier's accepted patches are smaller than CoEP's on shared instances (0.093 vs. 0.115), top_k's break-even is 704 vs. a 108-submission offering, and a patch-retrieval bound (33% near-duplicate coverage vs. rules' 50.7%).

**S5. Statistical honesty above the norm.** The paper itself flags that hybrid-vs-baseline McNemar is structurally one-sided, that submissions cluster in 5 assignments, and that the cluster-level sign test on the headline edge (3 wins, 0 losses, 2 ties -> p = 0.25, which I confirm) is not significant — "consistent but suggestive" is the correct description, stated in both the abstract and internal-validity discussion.

**S6. The safety property is real and correctly architected.** Synthesized repairers cannot self-report success (independent re-validation of every patch; test-pass + cap), so the observed failure mode is abstention only — 0 unsafe outputs in 200 attempts — which is what makes the inheritance argument structural rather than empirical.

### Weaknesses

**W1. The hidden-oracle audit's 4.5pp differential is disclosed but never propagated to the headline claims — and propagating it materially changes them.** The audit reports 92.6% of accepted local patches agree with the reference on all probes vs. 97.1% of the baseline's; the paper's primary metric (visible-test pass + cap) is the very objective CoEP's search optimizes directly. Carrying the audit rates through: local 95 x 0.926 ≈ 88 semantically clean; baseline 172 x 0.971 ≈ 167 (83.5%); hybrid ≈ 88 + 85 x 0.971 ≈ 170.5 (85.3%). The headline 4.0pp quality edge (90.0 vs. 86.0, p = 0.008) shrinks to ~1.8pp with mixed discordance — the significance almost certainly evaporates. Worse, the "exact inheritance" claim inherits the asterisk: CoEP+retry ≈ 95 x 0.926 + 102 x 0.971 ≈ 187 (93.5%) vs. retry ≈ 197 x 0.971 ≈ 191 (95.7%) — i.e., under the paper's own audit numbers, "identical quality at 1.8x lower cost" becomes "~2pp lower semantic quality at 1.8x lower cost", a trade-off, not a free lunch. The paper's proposed mitigation (add the probes as a third zero-LLM-cost validator and escalate probe failures) would restore true inheritance at modestly lower coverage (~44%, marginal cost ~$1.53 vs. $1.43 against the frontier fallback) — but this configuration was not run. This is the single most important unaddressed issue.

**W2. With 5 clusters, no cluster-level comparison in the paper can be significant, including the ablation.** A two-sided sign test at the assignment level maxes out at p = 0.0625 even for a 5:0 sweep; the headline edge is 3:0:2 (p = 0.25). All pooled Wilson CIs and McNemar tests treat 200 submissions (or 1,660) as independent, but local coverage ranges 27.9–71.4% across assignments; a cluster-robust CI on the 50.7% figure would be on the order of +/-20pp, not +/-2.4pp. The CIs are defensible only for fixed-corpus inference ("these five assignments"), and the paper should say so explicitly rather than only flagging clustering for the paired tests. The submission-level p < 10^-24 for the ablation is fine as a description of this corpus; as evidence about assignments in general, n = 5.

**W3. One stochastic builder trajectory per assignment; no seed replication of the synthesis step.** The central artifact is a stochastic LLM product, and the key quantities riding on single draws include: 47.5% coverage, the 2-of-5 one-shot / 3-of-5 rescued-by-refinement split, per-assignment break-evens (38–704), and the RQ3 narrative. At $1.28 per full replication, 3–5 seeds were affordable; "multi-seed replication is mechanical with the released harness" concedes the gap without closing it. The Haiku-builder rerun probes model capability, not seed variance.

**W4. Absolute dollar stakes at the demonstrated scale are trivial, and the paper's framing outruns them.** Using the paper's own numbers: gross saving vs. the plain baseline = (2.73−1.43)x10^-3 x 337 submissions ≈ $0.44 per assignment per offering, ~$0.18 net of build; at retry parity, ≈ $0.50 gross. "Fundamentally improving the cost structure" (abstract) is a slope-vs-intercept argument that is technically correct but, at CS1 scale, is worth cents; the practically compelling benefits (sub-second local latency, no rate limits, deadline-spike robustness, auditability) are operational, not monetary, and are argued rather than measured (e.g., no deadline-spike experiment). MOOC-scale economics are extrapolated, not demonstrated.

**W5. Quantitative claims in summary boxes exceed what the text supports.**
(a) RQ2 box: "inside one offering for four of five assignments" — the text establishes three of five (38 < 557, 88 < 417, 241 < 306; sort_age 368 > 297 needs ~1.2 offerings; top_k 704 vs. 108). I find no reading that yields four.
(b) "1.8–2.5x smaller cost slope" (RQ2 box, Conclusion): like-for-like fronting is bounded by 1/(1−coverage) = 1.90 and measures 1.81–1.91x (1.94->1.07, 3.26->1.78, 2.73->1.43); 2.55x is the *cross-protocol* comparison 2.73/1.07 (plain frontier vs. CoEP+cascade), where the quality set is not "preserved" but different. The Conclusion attaches "1.8–2.5x" to the matched-98.5% claim, where the true figure is 1.83x.
(c) Intro: "recouped after ~197 repaired submissions" — N* = C/Δ counts *processed* submissions (Δ is the per-submission saving including escalations), not repaired ones.

**W6. All dollar figures are modeled, not billed, via a chars/3.6 tokenizer approximation — despite the arms having different input/output and code/prose mixes.** The paper's uniform-error-cancels argument is fine for ratios, and the +/-20% differential sensitivity statement is roughly right for break-evens, but real LLM calls were made through a harness; provider-reported usage should have been recorded at call time. One consequence I checked does survive: the sub-1,024-token claim is robust (439 approx-tokens ≈ 1,580 chars cannot plausibly exceed 1,024 real tokens). However, the rhetorical claim that caching "cannot even engage" is not quite right: padding the shared prefix to the 1,024-token minimum makes it engage at ≈ $2.31x10^-3 by my arithmetic (~15% below plain) — this does not threaten CoEP's position ($1.43), but the anatomy section's absolutism should be tempered.

**W7. A natural zero-build competitor is missing: Refactory-tool + cascade fallback.** Refactory+frontier already achieves 86.5% at $1.33x10^-3 — cheaper than CoEP+frontier ($1.43) with zero build cost. Composing the tool with the cascade (as CoEP is composed) would plausibly land ~88–89% at ≈$0.95–1.0x10^-3, undercutting CoEP+cascade's cost at ~2pp lower quality. The paper's real defense — the tool requires 2,442 correct student solutions, unavailable for a fresh assignment, vs. CoEP's 3 refs + 5 bugs — is made, but the missing arm leaves the "lowest marginal cost of any quality-competitive arm" claim resting on an incomplete grid.

**W8. Contamination symmetry covers the paired comparisons, not the absolute coverage estimate.** The corpus (bugs and fixes) has been public since 2019; a builder that has absorbed this exact bug distribution may synthesize better rules for *these* assignments than for novel ones, so 47.5%/50.7% coverage — and hence every break-even — may be optimistic off-corpus. The paper's counter-evidence (refinement needed for formatting defects; Haiku-builder collapse) argues against verbatim fix memorization but not against distributional familiarity. Combined with 5 assignments, one course, one language, one model family, external validity is the paper's thinnest dimension, and the authors say so.

**W9. Minor: the tau-sweep concedes the hybrid tracks or exceeds the plain baseline only for tau >= 0.25**, with a conservativeness argument below (frozen repairers searched under 0.35 — sound reasoning, and the same lower-bound logic applies to the retry arm, but the claimed dominance is thus verified on part of the range only); figure contents (tau-sweep, cumulative cost) are not independently checkable from the submission; decoding temperature and exact model snapshot IDs ("July 2026 snapshots") are unstated.

## 3. Questions for the Authors

1. **(Most important.)** Recompute the headline comparisons under the hidden-oracle-augmented metric (visible pass AND cap AND probe agreement): (a) hybrid vs. plain baseline — rates, discordance, exact p; (b) CoEP+retry vs. retry — how many of the 95 locally patched instances fail probes where the retry arm's own patch passes? My propagation of your 92.6%/97.1% figures suggests the 4.0pp edge shrinks to ~1.8pp and the "identical quality" inheritance becomes ~2pp semantic regression. Also: were the audit rates measured on the 95/172 evaluation-set patches, and can you run the probe-as-third-validator configuration you propose?
2. Please reconcile the RQ2 box's "inside one offering for four of five assignments" with the text's three of five, and state precisely which comparison "2.5x" refers to, given like-for-like fronting is bounded by 1/(1−0.475) = 1.90x.
3. Can you report frontier-builder variance across >= 3 synthesis seeds per assignment (~$4 total): coverage, build cost, and refinement-round counts? Which of the per-assignment break-evens (38–704) are stable across seeds?
4. Why were provider-reported token counts not recorded at call time, given live API calls were made? Please state the exact model snapshot IDs and decoding parameters (temperature, top-p, max tokens).
5. Was the hidden-oracle audit run on all arms (retry, cascade, tool) or only CoEP-local and the plain baseline? Report denominators and a CI or test for the 92.6 vs. 97.1 differential.
6. Why is Refactory-tool + cascade fallback absent from Table 2? Please add it or justify its exclusion beyond the correct-solution-availability argument.
7. Which assignments anchor the 27.9% and 71.4% local-coverage extremes, and is the claim that "build-round trajectories signal [coverage] before deployment" anything more than an observation over 5 points?
8. Doesn't padding the shared prefix past the 1,024-token cache minimum (≈$2.31x10^-3 by my arithmetic) contradict "caching cannot even engage"? Please temper or rebut.
9. Given the retry protocol needs the same local validator infrastructure CoEP needs, and the net dollar saving at your scale is ~$0.2–0.5 per assignment-offering, is the deployment case ultimately latency/autonomy rather than dollars — and can you measure the deadline-spike scenario you invoke?

## 4. Significance

**3/4.** The reframing — amortize LLM reasoning into a verifiable per-assignment executable rather than optimizing per-query cost — is a clean, genuinely useful deployment insight for any many-instances-one-specification workload, and the paper positions it correctly against caching, cascades, and retrieval (each shown to compose with, not substitute for, it). Significance is capped by scale: five CS1 Python assignments from one 2019 corpus, absolute dollar stakes of cents per assignment-offering at demonstrated volumes, and education-specific constraints (patch-size cap) doing real work in the headline comparisons.

## 5. Justification of the Approach

**3/4.** Each design decision is well-motivated and mostly validated: the RPS cap operationalizes a real pedagogical requirement (with a tau-sweep), never-trust-self-reports validation makes the hybrid's non-inferiority structural, the refinement loop is shown to matter (3/5 builders rescued), and the operator library vs. synthesized rules division is cleanly ablated. Two gaps keep this from a 4: the synthesis step's stochastic robustness is asserted rather than measured (single trajectory per assignment), and the choice of the visible test suite as the local search's direct objective — the source of the differential overfitting the audit reveals — is acknowledged but not designed around, despite the authors themselves identifying a zero-cost fix (probe validator) they did not run.

## 6. Quality of the Evaluation

**3/4.** High end of 3. Same-model control, enforcement-parity baseline, adverse-selection-aware cost sums, exact tests with disclosed cluster caveats, full-corpus local re-run, oracle cross-check of the ported tool, and an internally consistent number set that survives full recomputation (S1, S3). Held back from 4 by: the unpropagated hidden-oracle differential that touches the headline and inheritance claims (W1), submission-level-only inference with 5 clusters presented with tight pooled CIs (W2), single-seed synthesis (W3), a missing natural competitor arm (W7), and modeled rather than billed costs (W6).

## 7. Reproducibility

**3/4.** Claimed release is comprehensive: harness, import pipeline, prompts, synthesized repairers, every builder round's prompt/response for both tiers, raw strings for re-tokenization, seeds and thresholds fixed and stated; the break-even is closed-form in re-substitutable quantities. Missing: exact model snapshot identifiers and decoding parameters; figures are not regenerable from the text alone; the LLM-dependent steps (builder synthesis, per-submission arms) are inherently non-replayable once the July 2026 snapshots age, which released raw outputs mitigate but do not eliminate. I could not inspect the supplementary artifact itself.

## 8. Ethical Considerations

No serious concerns. Data are public, de-identified student submissions from a standard benchmark; the system produces feedback, not grades, under stated instructor oversight; the escalation design avoids denying weaker students service. One note the authors should add: locally auto-generated "repairs" that pass visible tests but are semantically wrong (the audit's 7.4% of local patches) are shown to students as feedback — the probe validator they propose should be framed as a student-facing safeguard, not merely a metric.

## 9. Overall Evaluation

**7/10 (accept).** This is the rare cost paper whose arithmetic all reproduces, whose strongest baseline is one the authors built to beat themselves, and whose statistical caveats are largely self-reported. The load-bearing result — the identical 197/200 quality set at 1.83x lower marginal cost, break-even ~173 submissions, plus a structural non-inferiority guarantee — survives my adversarial recomputation with one real asterisk: propagating the paper's own hidden-oracle differential turns "same quality, less cost" into "~2pp lower semantic quality, less cost", and deflates the headline 90.0-vs-86.0 significance; the summary boxes also contain a four-vs-three-of-five error and an inflated 2.5x range. These are fixable (one cheap additional run, plus prose corrections), the paradigm contribution is real, and the evaluation hygiene is well above the acceptance bar; the modest demonstrated scale and single-seed synthesis keep it from 8.

**Confidence: 4/5.** I recomputed the cost model, break-evens, intervals, and all significance tests from the submission and verified cross-table consistency; I know the cost-efficient-inference and APR literatures well. I could not inspect the figures, the supplementary artifact, or the raw outputs.
