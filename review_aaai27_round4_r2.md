# AAAI-27 Review — Round 4, Reviewer 2

**Paper:** Use LLMs to Build Repair Tools, Not Patch Generation: Cost-Effective Automated Program Repair (CoEP)
**Reviewer specialty:** ML systems / cost-efficient inference; experimental rigor, cost accounting, statistics

---

## 1. Summary

The paper argues that LLM-based repair of programming-assignment submissions is deployed at the wrong granularity: every per-submission query re-transmits the assignment specification and tests (77% of input tokens byte-identical across a class), so cost scales linearly with enrollment. CoEP instead spends the LLM once per assignment: a builder agent reads the spec, tests, 3 reference solutions, and 5 sample bugs, and synthesizes an executable symbolic repairer (problem-specific AST rewrite rules over a generic bounded edit search), refined for up to 3 validation-feedback rounds, then deployed locally at zero marginal LLM cost with escalation of abstentions to conventional per-submission repair. On 5 assignments and 1,685 real student submissions from the public Refactory corpus, with the same frontier model in all LLM arms, the local repairers alone fix 47.5% of a 200-submission held-out sample (50.7% on the full 1,660-submission corpus); the hybrid reaches 90.0–90.5% "quality" repairs (test-pass and relative patch size <= 0.35) versus 86.0% for the plain per-submission baseline, and matches an enforced-retry baseline exactly (98.5%) at 1.8x lower marginal cost. The one-off build cost of $0.256/assignment breaks even at roughly 154–295 submissions, within one course offering for four of five assignments. Ablations attribute 86% of local repairs to the synthesized rules (generic search alone: 6.5%), show refinement rounds rescue 3 of 5 builders, and show a cheap-model builder collapses to 10%.

## 2. Strengths and Weaknesses

### Strengths

**S1. The arithmetic is correct — I recomputed essentially all of it.** This is rarer than it should be. Baseline cost (439 in, 94 out at $3/$15/Mtok) = $2.727e-3, matching 2.73. Build cost (51,227 in + 75,015 out) = $1.279 total, $0.256/assignment, = 93.9 baseline repairs ("~94"). Break-evens: 0.256/(2.73-1.43)e-3 = 197.4 ("197"); cascade-fronted vs. frontier = 154.5 ("~154"); vs. cascade = 294.3 ("~295"); retry-matched = 173.0 ("~173"); builder-upgrade = 228.8 ("~230"). All nine Wilson CIs in Table 1 and the full-corpus CI ([48.3, 53.1] at 842/1660) reproduce to the decimal. All exact McNemar p-values reproduce: 8:0 discordant -> 0.0078 ("0.008"); 9:0 -> 0.0039 ("0.004"); 82:0 -> 4.14e-25 ("4.1e-25"); 4:0 -> 0.125, honestly reported as n.s. The internal set algebra is airtight: local 95 = 87 shared with baseline + 8 unique; baseline 172 = 87 + 85; hybrid 180 = 95 + 85; generic 13 is a subset of local 95, giving discordant 82 and the "86% of everything" claim (82/95 = 86.3%).

**S2. Unusually honest evaluation design.** The paper builds and foregrounds the baseline that beats its own headline: validator + one retry lifts the per-submission baseline to 98.5% > 90.5%, and this appears in the abstract, intro, and Table 2, not buried. The plain-prompt caching row is explicitly labeled a "no-minimum bound unavailable in practice." A non-significant comparison (CoEP+cascade vs. cascade, 4:0) is labeled n.s. The local arm's safety property (zero test-failing, zero over-cap patches across 200 attempts; failure mode is only abstention) is a genuine, checkable design guarantee. The full-corpus re-run of the local arm (n=1,660, feasible because it is free) tightly confirms the subsample estimate (50.7% vs. 47.5%, inside the subsample CI).

**S3. Broad and fair baseline coverage under one harness.** Eight arms spanning the design space: plain/cached/few-shot/enforced-retry per-submission repair, cheap model, FrugalGPT-style cascade, generic-search-only ablation, and the non-LLM Refactory tool in its strongest configuration with 2,442 correct solutions (orders of magnitude more per-assignment data than CoEP receives). The same frontier model drives both paradigms; the few-shot arm is given information parity with the builder (same B_build students); Refactory's own oracle (91.0%) is cross-checked against the harness (90.5% test-pass), pinning its quality gap entirely on patch size.

**S4. The claim structure is right for a cost paper.** The composability result — fronting any fallback protocol preserves its quality set (guaranteed by the local arm's abstain-only failure mode) while diverting ~half the traffic — is the load-bearing claim, and it is construction-backed rather than significance-backed. The break-even algebra is closed-form with all inputs published, and the honest boundary (top_k, 108 submissions, does not break even in one offering) is stated.

**S5. Diagnostic, mechanism-level ablations.** RQ3 is not just numbers: the ast.unparse reformatting defect (0/5 -> 5/5 after switching to surgical line edits), the delivery-vs-comprehension distinction, and the Haiku-builder failure taxonomy (whole-file reformatting, unvalidated candidates) make the refinement-loop contribution concrete and support the contamination rebuttal (a memorizing builder would not need rounds to fix formatting).

### Weaknesses

**W1. The statistical hole: every significant p-value in the paper comes from a structurally nested comparison evaluated at the submission level with only five clusters.** Because the hybrid contains the fallback and the local path never emits an unsafe patch, hybrid-vs-baseline discordance can only be k:0 — the McNemar test degenerates to "did the local path ever uniquely win?", and any k >= 6 is automatically "significant." The paper acknowledges the one-sidedness, but the deeper problem is clustering: submissions cluster within 5 assignments, and the 8 discordant instances are concentrated in 3 of them ("strictly better in three, tied in two"). Treating the assignment as the unit of inference, a sign test on 3 wins / 0 losses / 2 ties gives p = 0.25 — not significant. Even the massive 82:0 ablation is 5:0 at cluster level (p = 0.0625). Meanwhile, no non-nested comparison in the paper reaches significance (few-shot vs. plain: untested; cascade vs. plain: untested; CoEP+cascade vs. cascade: n.s.). The abstract, intro, and both RQ boxes headline "p=0.008" without the clustering caveat, which appears only in Metrics/Discussion as "we report per-assignment directions alongside." The honest inferential summary is: 8–9 unique wins on 200 subsampled instances, concentrated in 3 of 5 assignments, never reversed. That is suggestive, not the demonstrated quality superiority the abstract's "exceeding ... (exact McNemar p=0.008)" conveys. A cluster-robust analysis (permutation over assignments, cluster bootstrap, or mixed-effects logistic) is absent, and with 5 clusters it likely cannot reach significance — which is an argument for more assignments, not for submission-level p-values.

**W2. The headline quality win over the plain baseline is an artifact of a deployment convention the paper itself shows is trivially repairable.** One validator-triggered retry lifts the baseline from 86.0% to 98.5%, far above the hybrid's 90.5%. The baseline's deficit is not inability but the absence of enforcement — as the paper says. Consequently "exceeding the per-submission frontier baseline" (abstract) and "exceeds every deployed single-shot configuration" (RQ1 box, where "single-shot" quietly excludes the two-shot retry arm) are technically true but promotional; the defensible claim is the cost claim (same 98.5% at $1.78 vs. $3.26 per thousand submissions). The paper does also make the defensible claim prominently, but the abstract leads with the fragile one. Relatedly, "1.8–2.5x" mixes same-fallback ratios (2.73/1.43 = 1.91; 3.26/1.78 = 1.83; 1.94/1.07 = 1.81) with a cross-arm pairing (2.73/1.07 = 2.55); at matched 98.5% quality the ratio is 1.8x, not 2.5x, yet the conclusion attaches the full range to the retry match.

**W3. Composite-arm cost figures are not recomputable from stated quantities, and the discrepancies are unexplained in-paper.** Cascade: 0.90 + 0.35 x 2.73 = $1.86e-3, table says 1.94 (+4%). CoEP+cascade: 0.525 x 1.94 = $1.02e-3, table says 1.07 (+5%). CoEP+retry: 0.525 x 3.26 = $1.71e-3, table says 1.78 (+4%). Few-shot + caching: a contiguous-prefix computation with the quoted 1,493 identical tokens gives $2.49e-3, table says 2.91 — implying an effective cacheable prefix of ~1,339 tokens, never stated. Cached-plain: my bound is 2.08, table says 2.10. These gaps are all in the plausible direction (fallback instances are harder, so they escalate more; identical tokens are not all contiguous), and their sign is conservative against CoEP, which is to the authors' credit — but a cost paper should print the per-arm cost formula and the measured escalation rates so a reviewer does not have to reverse-engineer them. Note also that CoEP+frontier is exactly 0.525 x 2.73, i.e., computed as a product, while the cascade arms apparently are not — the accounting convention is inconsistent or under-documented. Finally, "a +/-20% differential calibration error shifts break-even points by at most the same factor" is loose: opposing 20% errors on C and Delta compound to ~1.5x, and Delta is a difference, so error propagation is not multiplicative in general.

**W4. One stochastic builder trajectory per assignment; the unit economics depend on a one-draw random variable.** Local coverage ranges 27.9–71.4% across assignments, and the Haiku-builder ablation (47.5% -> 10.0%) shows synthesis outcome is highly sensitive to the builder. With n=1 build per assignment, the variance of the headline 47.5%/50.7% across builder seeds — and hence of every break-even N* — is unquantified. "Multi-seed replication is mechanical with the released harness" concedes the gap without closing it; at $0.256 per build, five replications would have cost ~$6.40. Relatedly, the break-even claims use corpus-average coverage and average build cost; per-assignment N* is not tabulated, and at 27.9% coverage the frontier-fallback break-even is ~336 submissions, above two of the five assignments' single-offering volumes — the "four of five inside one offering" claim is average-economics, not per-assignment economics.

**W5. Visible-test oracle only, with an acknowledged asymmetry that favors CoEP.** Quality = passing the instructor's visible tests within the cap; CoEP's local search optimizes that oracle directly (first_passing returns the first suite-passing candidate), while LLM arms generate semantically. The RPS cap bounds only the degenerate overfit (wholesale replacement), not test-suite-overfitted small edits. The paper admits "suite overfitting favors it differentially; hidden-test evaluation is the natural next control" — but does not run the control, though mutation-based or held-out tests were feasible. For a pedagogy-motivated quality metric this is the second-biggest evaluation gap after W1. Note also that on the intensive margin the baseline's accepted patches are smaller (0.093 vs. 0.115 RPS on the 87 shared instances) — CoEP's advantage is purely extensive (enforcement), which the paper does disclose.

**W6. Tau and the sweep.** The cap value tau = 0.35 "follows Refactory and PyDex," but those works use patch-size metrics, not (to my knowledge) a 0.35 accept/reject threshold; the value's provenance (fixed before results?) is unstated, and the entire quality story lives on the cap (at tau = infinity all frontier arms are 100% and the hybrid's advantage vanishes). The tau-sweep is the right mitigation and is honestly caveated (hybrid drops below baseline for tau < 0.25 in the recomputation; local repairers searched under 0.35, so their curves are lower bounds). But the sweep is also not policy-faithful for the retry arm: retries were triggered by 0.35-violations, so at other tau the recomputed retry curve is not the protocol that would have run — the "retry dominates at every tau" claim is directionally safe (the recomputation lower-bounds it) but should be stated as such. Contamination is argued to be symmetric across LLM arms; the residual asymmetry the rebuttal does not cover is that the builder's task (anticipating this corpus's bug distribution) benefits from corpus-level memorization of the publicly released buggy submissions themselves, beyond the 5 samples shown — the delivery-defect evidence addresses form, not rule content. A private or post-cutoff assignment would settle it.

**W7. Minor.** (a) "+/-5pp Wilson intervals" oversells: at 47.5%/200 the half-width is 6.9pp. (b) numbers.tex hygiene: \NumBuggy=225 contradicts the 1,685 used in the text (macro unused, so no rendered error); \BaseMeanRPS=0.187 equals the Refactory row of Table 1, not the baseline's 0.102; \BreakEvenNCached and \AsymReduction are stale — version-drift risk in a paper whose credibility rests on numerical consistency. (c) "properties per-query paradigms cannot offer at any price" — a locally hosted open model offers latency/rate-limit independence too; rhetorical overreach. (d) The natural zero-LLM amortization competitor — retrieve/reuse patches from near-duplicate prior submissions — is dismissed in one parenthetical rather than measured.

## 3. Questions for the Authors

1. **Cluster-robust inference (most decision-relevant).** Report the per-assignment discordant counts underlying the 8:0 and 9:0 comparisons and a cluster-aware test (permutation over assignments, cluster bootstrap, or GLMM). Do any of the headline superiority claims survive at alpha = 0.05 when the assignment is the unit? If not, will you re-word the abstract/RQ boxes to the descriptive claim?
2. Was tau = 0.35 fixed before any LLM arm was executed (as the split seed was)? What is its provenance beyond "follows Refactory and PyDex," which report metrics, not caps?
3. Give the exact cost formula per composite arm and the measured escalation rates on fallback instances. Specifically reconcile: cascade 1.94 vs. 0.90 + 0.35 x 2.73 = 1.86; CoEP+cascade 1.07 vs. 0.525 x 1.94 = 1.02; few-shot+cached 2.91 vs. a 1,493-token contiguous-prefix bound of 2.49; cached-plain 2.10 vs. 2.08. Why is CoEP+frontier exactly the 0.525 x 2.73 product when the cascade arms are not?
4. Is CoEP+retry's "identical 197/200" set obtained by reusing the retry arm's stored per-instance responses on fallback submissions? If so, the 0:0 discordance is true by construction and the only empirical content is that the local 95 are a subset of the retry arm's 197 — please state this explicitly.
5. Builder variance: for at least one assignment (ideally remove_extras and top_k), run 5 builder seeds and report the spread of local coverage, build cost, and the implied N*. Does the 27.9–71.4% heterogeneity reflect assignments or trajectories?
6. Hidden-test control: on the local arm's 95 patches vs. the baseline's 172, what fraction remains correct under held-out or mutation-generated tests? Even a small study would address the differential-overfitting concern you acknowledge.
7. Tabulate per-assignment break-even N* using per-assignment coverage and build cost. For which assignments does amortization pay off within their observed single-offering volume?
8. Why is patch reuse across near-duplicate submissions (a retrieval index over previously repaired programs, zero LLM cost after warm-up) not an evaluated baseline, given MOOC-scale duplication is central to your economics argument?

## 4. Significance

**Score: 3/4.** The many-instances-one-specification framing and the amortize-reasoning-into-a-verifiable-artifact recipe are genuinely useful, sit in an active AAAI lineage (Refactory/Verifix through AAAI-25/26 per-submission work), and the economics are worked out with unusual care, including the honest boundary where they fail (low-traffic assignments). It is a deployment-economics contribution rather than a technical advance — the components (self-refine loops, template repair, test validation, cascades) are known, and the quality gains are convention-dependent (W2) — so it falls short of the top rating.

## 5. Justification of the Approach

**Score: 3/4.** The design is well-motivated and internally principled: never-trust-self-reports validation, abstain-only failure, escalation preserving coverage, and the break-even model are the right skeleton, and the cost-anatomy section justifies why caching cannot fix the structure (prompts below the 1,024-token cacheable minimum). Under-justified knobs: one retry as "enforcement parity" (CoEP's local search enumerates unboundedly many validated candidates; the retry arm gets exactly one), the tau value's provenance, k=5 build bugs, and R_max=3 — all plausible, none derived or sensitivity-tested except builder tier.

## 6. Quality of the Evaluation

**Score: 3/4.** On the credit side: same-model discipline, eight fair baselines including the strongest configuration of the non-LLM competitor, information-parity few-shot, an adversarial-to-the-paper retry arm, correct Wilson/McNemar arithmetic throughout (verified), a free full-corpus confirmation of the local arm, and released raw strings for re-tokenization. On the debit side: submission-level inference over five clusters where cluster-level tests would be non-significant (W1), a single builder trajectory per assignment (W4), visible-test oracle with acknowledged differential overfitting risk and no hidden-test control (W5), composite cost figures not recomputable from the paper (W3), and head-to-head comparisons restricted to a 200-instance subsample. The evaluation is honest and extensive but its inferential layer is fragile in exactly the dimension the paper's headline emphasizes.

## 7. Reproducibility

**Score: 3/4.** Strong: seeded scripted splits from a public corpus, released prompts/repairers/raw responses for both builder tiers, deterministic local search, fixed tokenizer convention with released strings for exact re-pricing, CPU-only, thorough checklist. Withheld from 4: exact model snapshot identifiers appear only as "July 2026 snapshots" in the text (deferred to the released configuration), per-arm cost formulas and escalation rates are not in the paper (W3), figures' generation inputs are not described, and the artifact itself could not be inspected in this review. The stale numbers.tex macros (W7b) are a small but real drift hazard for anyone rebuilding the paper's numbers.

## 8. Ethical Considerations

No substantive concerns. Data are public, de-identified student submissions from a standard research corpus; the system automates formative feedback, not grading, under instructor oversight, and unrepaired students receive the full-LLM path, so no learner is disadvantaged by the cost optimization. Two soft points the authors could note: (i) synthesized repairers should be audited before deployment since rules encode assumptions about "misconceptions" that could mis-patch unconventional-but-valid approaches (the test suite and RPS cap mitigate); (ii) pedagogical claims about patch minimality being better feedback are cited but not evaluated with learners — the paper appropriately does not claim a learning-outcome effect.

## 9. Overall Evaluation

**Overall: 6/10 (marginal accept).**

This is a carefully executed, honestly reported systems-economics paper whose every number I checked reproduces, whose strongest counter-baseline (validated retry at 98.5%) the authors built themselves and placed in the abstract, and whose central cost claim — same quality set at a 1.8x smaller slope, break-even inside one course offering — is construction-backed and robust. Against that: the headline quality-superiority claim rests on 8 discordant instances in a 5-cluster design where cluster-aware inference yields p ~= 0.25, every significant p-value in the paper is structurally guaranteed to be one-directional, the quality win over the plain baseline is an artifact of a one-shot convention the paper itself shows one retry repairs, the build is a single stochastic draw per assignment, and the oracle is the visible suite the local search directly optimizes. None of these is fatal, most are at least acknowledged, and the cost contribution stands independently of W1–W2 — but the inferential over-claiming in the abstract and RQ boxes, and the missing cluster-robust/multi-seed/hidden-test analyses, keep this below a clear accept. I would raise to 7 if the authors (a) report cluster-level analyses and re-scope the superiority language accordingly, (b) publish the composite-arm cost formulas reconciling the gaps in Q3, and (c) add even a small multi-seed build study.

**Confidence: 4/5.** I recomputed the cost model, break-evens, confidence intervals, and significance tests myself and know the cost-efficient-inference and APR literatures well; I did not execute the artifact or inspect the figures/supplementary, and my cluster-level reanalysis relies on the paper's own per-assignment direction reports.
